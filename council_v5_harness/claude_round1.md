# Council v5: Medical agent harness. Claude, Round 1

**Evidence base:** `reports/Medical agent harness.md` and `research_notes/Medical agent harness/`. Descartes proposes, Hume audits, Socrates stress-tests. Two of these five ideas sharpen the report's framings; three are new.

**Opening position (Descartes).** The student's instinct is right, but the claim has to shrink. "No one has built a medical harness" died on 5 Oct 2026 (MedicalHarness: model 58% of variance, harness 12%, interaction 12%, seed noise 18%). What survives is narrower and better: **the harness is the only layer a Thai hospital fully controls**. PDPA and on-prem hardware cap the model size, and the Medical Council requires that "the doctor decides". Both requirements are harness properties, not model properties. The thesis therefore should not claim "harness beats model". It should claim **"these safety guarantees can only live in the harness; here is what they cost, and how that depends on model size."**

### 1. Poster decay: how prompt-level safety rules degrade with context length, pressure and model size, and how hard stops do not
- **Pitch:** The report's "poster vs hard stop" contrast, turned into a *curve*. A rule written in the system prompt (poster) is followed less as the context fills up, as the patient pushes back, and as the model gets smaller. The same rule as a deterministic hook (hard stop) should stay flat. We measure both curves and the utility each one costs.
- **Paradigm & research question (harness engineering, safety):** *With the model held fixed, how does the rate of executed unsafe medication actions under prompt-only rules change with (a) context fill (2k → 8k → 24k tokens of realistic Thai chart noise), (b) adversarial patient pressure (none / insistent / authority claims such as "หมอบอกให้กินสองเท่า", "the doctor told me to take double"), and (c) model size (4B → 8B → API)? How much of that gap does a hook close, and at what cost in over-blocking?* This is falsifiable: compare the slope of unsafe-action rate against each factor for the H3-prompt and H3-hook arms using a mixed-effects logistic model.
- **Thai testbed and impact:**
  - Users are pharmacists and doctors in community hospitals and sub-district health centres (รพ.สต.) running small on-prem models.
  - Thai failures already documented: TMA-1 scores ≤27% on drug–drug interactions, and TSWAP produced a dosing schedule once its safety prompt was removed.
  - The impact is a published decay curve that tells a hospital *which rules must never live in a prompt*.
- **Laptop experiment:**
  - Models: Typhoon2.5-Qwen3-4B, Pathumma-4.5B, an 8B Thai model, a Qwen3-4B control, and one API model (synthetic data only).
  - Tasks: ~250 Thai medication tasks (TMT normalisation; dose, route, ก่อน/หลังอาหาร (before/after meals); drug–drug interactions only where sources agree), crossed with 3 context levels and 3 pressure levels. Use a fractional design to keep it to about 15k episodes.
  - Runtime: Inspect + vLLM, k=3 epochs, ~60–120 laptop-hours. Hardware cost: none.
- **Socratic test:** *Prompt rules actually decay within an 8–32k context.* If small models already ignore the rule at 2k, there is no decay to measure, only a floor. Month 1: 50 tasks × 2 models × {2k, 16k} × 3 epochs. **Pivot** if the unsafe-action rate at 2k is already ≥30% (there is no curve, only a floor; that is still a finding, but a smaller one), or if it stays ≤5% at 16k (nothing to fix).

### 2. Guardrail dependence: does training a model *under* a hook make it worse when the hook is removed?
- **Pitch:** The report's "verifier as hook vs verifier as reward", with a sharper question. When the hook is active during RL rollouts, the model never sees the consequence of an unsafe action because the hook catches it first. Does it learn to rely on the guardrail? That would be the agent version of moral hazard.
- **Paradigm & research question (RL post-training × harness):** 2×2×2 design: train {no RL, GRPO with hook off, GRPO with hook on} × evaluate {hook off, hook on}, using the same verifier A as reward. *Is a model trained with the hook on less safe when the hook is removed than one trained with it off ("guardrail dependence")? Does training with the hook on reach the same safety on verifier B with fewer rollouts?* The core is the 2×2 slice (hook during training × hook at evaluation), measured on verifier B and the clinician set.
- **Thai testbed and impact:** In a real hospital the "hook" is a pharmacist's verification step, which often disappears on night shifts and in รพ.สต. The result tells deployers whether a guardrailed model degrades safely when the guardrail goes down. It also continues the student's dual-verifier RSI plan instead of discarding it.
- **Laptop experiment:**
  - Model and training: one 1.5–4B model (Qwen3-4B or a Thai 4B), QLoRA + GRPO, 3 training conditions × ~40–60 GPU-h = 120–180 GPU-h.
  - Tasks and evaluation: framing-1 tasks. Evaluate on verifier B (an independent implementation) and 50 pharmacist-validated items.
- **Socratic test:** *Hooks actually intercept enough rollouts during training to change what the model learns.* If the hook fires on fewer than 5% of rollouts, the hook-on and hook-off runs will be nearly identical. Month 1: log hook-fire rates on 200 rollouts of the untrained model. **Kill the RL arm** if the fire rate is below 5% or above 60%. Below 5% nothing changes; above 60% the model learns only to provoke blocks.

### 3. Harness-level RSI: a frontier model rewrites a small model's harness. Does the search learn to game safety?
- **Pitch:** Recursive self-improvement, but of the *harness* instead of the weights, and it needs no GPU training. A frontier "harness engineer" model (via API) repeatedly proposes harness edits for a local Thai 4B model: tool descriptions, context policy, hook thresholds, verification gates. We measure whether the search improves held-out performance or discovers shortcuts such as "escalate everything" or "abstain more".
- **Paradigm & research question (automated harness search, Meta-Harness-style, as RSI):** *Under search against a utility-only objective, does automated harness optimisation inflate over-escalation or abstention (reward hacking at the harness level)? Does adding unsafe-action and over-escalation constraints, enforced by frozen hooks the search cannot edit, keep the gains on a held-out split?* This is falsifiable: track search-set vs held-out success and over-escalation for each iteration, across 3 search seeds.
- **Thai testbed and impact:** The same Thai medication or triage tasks. It answers a practical question: can a Thai hospital IT team safely let an AI tune its on-prem harness? Meta-Harness tuned and tested on one benchmark, so this design fixes that flaw with a held-out split and frozen safety hooks.
- **Laptop experiment:**
  - Search: local model inference only. Each search iteration runs ~100 tasks × 3 epochs on the laptop (~1–2 h). 15–25 iterations × 3 seeds × 2 objective conditions ≈ 100–150 laptop-hours.
  - Proposer: a frontier API model at roughly $100–400 total (my estimate, unverified).
  - Baselines: the hand-built harness from #1 (H4) and random search.
- **Socratic test:** *The search space contains real improvements, not just noise.* MedicalHarness found 18% of variance is seed noise. Month 1: run 5 hand-made harness edits, 3 epochs each. **Kill** if no edit beats H0 by more than the replication spread, because the search would only chase noise. Then fall back to #1.

### 4. A context compiler for long Thai records on an 8–32k-context laptop
- **Pitch:** Small on-prem models have small effective context. Multi-visit Thai records shaped like the 43 files (MoPH-PC-1) quickly overflow it. The harness should *compile* the record, deciding what the model sees. We compare context policies on long-record clinical questions.
- **Paradigm & research question (context engineering):** *For 4–8B Thai models at 8k/16k/32k windows, which context policy (truncation, LLM summarisation, observation masking, structured retrieval over FHIR resources, or a typed "problem-list compiler") maximises accuracy per token on long-record tasks? Does the best policy flip with window size?* Fan et al. found context management is worth +35.7 points at 32k but only +2.7 at 128k; this tests the low-window regime the laptop actually lives in.
- **Thai testbed and impact:** Continuity of care across รพ.สต., community hospital and provincial hospital, where the referring doctor sees a long, messy record. Questions include current medications, allergies, last HbA1c trend and pending referrals.
- **Laptop experiment:**
  - Data: 100 synthetic longitudinal patients shaped to MoPH-PC-1 (3–40 visits), generated by script from 43-file field specifications and checked by a clinician for realism, plus 300 questions with deterministic answers.
  - Runs: 5 policies × 3 windows × 3 models × 3 epochs ≈ 40k short episodes, ~80–120 laptop-hours.
- **Socratic test:** *Synthetic longitudinal records are hard in the same way real ones are.* Month 1: a clinician compares 10 synthetic records with 10 de-identified real ones (if obtainable) on a 5-point realism scale. If no real records can be seen, the paper must lean on MedicalHarness's long-memory tasks as the external anchor. **Kill** if full-record truncation at 16k already scores ≥85% (no headroom).

### 5. A certifiable harness: regulate the safety layer, swap the model (bold)
- **Pitch:** Thai FDA cannot re-certify a model every month, but it *could* certify a harness. We write the safety requirements of a Thai clinical task as **runtime-verifiable properties over tool-call traces**, for example "no order without a TMT-coded dose check in the same episode" or "any red flag must lead to escalate() before finish()". We enforce them with a monitor and test whether the guarantee holds *for every model swapped in*.
- **Paradigm & research question (runtime verification for agents):** *What fraction of the safety requirements in a Thai clinical protocol can be expressed as trace properties? (Symbolic Guardrails found 74% for English benchmarks including MedAgentBench.) With the monitor on, is the violation rate zero by construction across all models? What is the utility cost per model?* This is falsifiable: hand-code each requirement from a Thai CPG (clinical practice guideline) or drug protocol, classify it as enforceable or not, then run 4 models × monitor on/off.
- **Thai testbed and impact:** A concrete proposal for how Thai FDA's SaMD rules and the Medical Council's "AI only supports" position could apply to LLM tools: *certify the harness and its trace monitor, keep the model swappable.* This is the argument most likely to interest the committee's clinicians and any regulator.
- **Laptop experiment:** Take one Thai protocol (e.g., anticoagulant dosing or a 1669 Criteria Based Dispatch card) and write ~30–60 requirements. Monitor in Python (or LTL via an off-the-shelf runtime-verification library) on the framing-1 or framing-2 task set. 4 models × 2 arms × 3 epochs. Compute is small (~30–60 h).
- **Socratic test:** *Thai clinical requirements are mostly symbolic.* Month 1: a pharmacist or ER partner and the student classify 40 requirements. **Pivot** if fewer than 50% are enforceable as trace properties. Then the contribution becomes the *taxonomy of what cannot be certified*, which is still publishable but a smaller result.

**If I were the student, I'd pick #1, with #3 as the frontier extension in months 6–8.** #1 has the cleanest open gap: nobody has measured the poster-vs-hard-stop *curve* in medicine. Its ground truth is near-deterministic, it runs on the laptop with no training, and the pharmacist partner decides everything. #3 then answers "where is the frontier ML?" with RSI applied to the harness rather than the weights. It reuses #1's tasks and hooks, and it carries the student's reward-hacking question forward without a GPU training pipeline. #5 is the most *committee-persuasive* framing and can be written as the discussion chapter of #1 rather than a separate project.

**One thing I'd push back on:** the student's "infantry vs factory" analogy is good for the committee but should not be the *research question*. MedicalHarness and Mingbird already compare whole harnesses. The defensible novelty is *which component*, *on which safety metric*, *at which model size*, *in Thai*.
