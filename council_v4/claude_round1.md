# Council v4: Claude, Round 1

**Scope:** physical AI, robotics, drones, AR/XR, and medical education. **Evidence base:** `reports/Thai physical AI and med education.md`. Descartes proposes, Hume audits, Socrates stress-tests. Two of these five ideas sharpen the report's top two; three are new.

### 1. ExpertDynamics: a latent world model of expert hands as an unsupervised skill critic
- **Pitch:** Learn how *expert* suturing evolves in a latent space. A trainee's skill is how badly that model predicts their next moments, and the moments where prediction fails are the feedback.
- **Paradigm & research question (JEPA / latent world model as an evaluator):** Supervised skill heads collapse across sites (CCC 0.78–0.81 on the training dataset, 0.06–0.20 on another, arXiv 2608.17519). *Does an expert-only, action-conditioned latent predictor give a skill signal that transfers across datasets and rubrics better than a supervised head on the same frozen features? And do its error peaks land where human raters say the errors are?* This is falsifiable: compare cross-dataset Spearman/CCC (AIxSuture → OSS → LASANA suture subset) with a supervised probe, and compare temporal overlap of error peaks with rater-marked error windows.
- **Thai testbed:** CHAMPS (Chula's simulation centre) part-task suturing for medical students and interns. The Thai bottleneck is instructor time (93.3% of administrators report a technician shortage; 74.4% of instructors report insufficient faculty). The outcome a clinician can read is *instructor-minutes per competent learner*.
- **Laptop experiment:**
  - Features: frozen V-JEPA 2 ViT-L features plus 21-keypoint hand trajectories (MediaPipe or a similar hand tracker), precomputed once (~20–40 GPU-h).
  - Predictor: a small (~10–30M) predictor trained only on top-quartile videos (~30–60 GPU-h per configuration).
  - Baselines: supervised regression head, VideoMAE features, Kinetics-only X3D.
  - Local data: 40–60 GoPro/phone recordings at CHAMPS, scored by 2 raters.
  - Total ~150–250 GPU-h.
- **Socratic test:** *Prediction error tracks skill rather than camera angle, suture-pad colour or speed.* Month 1: measure prediction error on expert videos under nuisance changes (crop, lighting, playback speed ×0.8/×1.2). **Kill or pivot** if nuisance changes move the error more than the gap between novices and experts, or if Spearman |ρ| with OSATS is below 0.3 on held-out AIxSuture.

### 2. SonoWorld: a probe-conditioned ultrasound world model that coaches FAST scanning
- **Pitch:** Train a latent world model of "move the probe this way, see this image" from CT-derived simulated ultrasound. Then use it to guide novice interns to the four FAST trauma views on a phantom, with arrows shown on a phone or in Quest 3 mixed reality.
- **Paradigm & research question (action-conditioned world model + planning, sim-to-real):** *Can a world model trained only on CT-to-ultrasound simulation plan probe corrections that cut novices' time-to-standard-view on a real phantom? Does planning in the world model beat a direct view-classifier "hot/cold" baseline?* The comparison is falsifiable as a small crossover (n≈16–24 novices; guided vs unguided; time-to-view and view-quality rated by an emergency physician blinded to condition).
- **Thai testbed:** FAST is the bedside trauma scan that rural CPIRD-track interns must perform alone. Thailand's road-traffic death rate is among the highest in the world (WHO estimate; verify the current figure). Point-of-care ultrasound teaching is limited by faculty time, the same bottleneck as #1.
- **Laptop experiment:**
  - Simulator: label-map ray-casting ultrasound from public CT (e.g., TotalSegmentator, CC BY), covering the abdomen and pericardium. CPU/GPU slice rendering is cheap.
  - Data: 1–2M simulated (pose, Δpose, image) transitions.
  - World model: ~50–100M latent model (~80–150 GPU-h). Planning by MPC/CEM in latent space (runs on the laptop).
  - Real evaluation: a FAST phantom and a handheld probe (Butterfly/Clarius-class; check what CHAMPS or the emergency department owns), tracked with an ArUco cube on the probe and a webcam.
- **Socratic test:** *Simulated ultrasound shares enough structure with real images that a sim-trained view recogniser works on the phantom.* Month 1: render 4 FAST views, train a view classifier in sim, and test on 200 real phantom frames. **Kill** if accuracy is below 60%. Also do a novelty check against Caption Health-style cardiac guidance, which is FDA-cleared prior art for echo; whether FAST view guidance exists is unverified.

### 3. Thai OSCE standardized patient trained by dual-verifier self-play
- **Pitch:** The audit-first RSI plan, moved into medical education. Small Thai LLM "patients" improve themselves through self-play against a rule-based case-fact verifier, and we measure the round at which they start to game it.
- **Paradigm & research question (verifier-guided self-play / RSI):**
  - Setup: Proposer (interviewer) and Solver (patient). Verifier A is rule-based: no invented findings, no volunteered diagnosis, reveal only what is asked. Verifier B is independent: clinician-rated coverage and realism, plus a held-out interview set.
  - *At which round does optimising against verifier A turn into reward hacking against B (an evasive or under-informative patient), and does an early-warning signal (divergence between A and B on a probe set) predict it?*
  - This is safer than the triage version: a mistake harms a student exercise, not a patient.
- **Thai testbed:** Step 3 of the licensing exam is a Thai-language OSCE (secondary source; verify with the exam body, CMA). Standardized patients are already used by 90.2% of schools and are expensive. The main LLM virtual-patient review excluded non-English studies.
- **Laptop experiment:** Qwen3-4B / Typhoon-class 4B, QLoRA + GRPO; 20 Thai OSCE cases authored with an emergency-medicine or health-professions-education clinician; 8–10 self-play rounds (~150–250 GPU-h). Use a text interface first; Thai voice is optional. Check tone-mark integrity under 4-bit quantisation, since a 2026 deployment saw 4-bit AWQ corrupt Thai tone marks.
- **Socratic test:** *Off-the-shelf models are not already good enough.* Month 1: run 3 open models through 10 scripted interviews per case. **Kill the reinforcement-learning part** if the best one already hallucinates no more than 5% and covers at least 90% of case facts. **Kill the verifier premise** if verifier A disagrees with the clinician on more than 20% of turns. Also check for an existing Chula–KBTG virtual patient.

### 4. ThaiScrub-VLA: does a small vision-language-action model listen to code-switched Thai operating-room speech? (bold)
- **Pitch:** A ~US$300 SO-101 arm passes instruments when asked the way Thai OR staff actually speak: Thai with English instrument names ("ขอ Kelly", "ส่ง Metzenbaum ให้หน่อย"). We test whether the policy actually uses the words.
- **Paradigm & research question (vision-language-action robustness):** VLAs are suspected of ignoring language when the visual scene predicts the action (a hypothesis to verify in the 2025–26 literature). *When several instruments look alike, does a fine-tuned SmolVLA ground code-switched Thai instructions? Does a language-contrastive augmentation (the same scene with a different instrument name) close the gap?* This is falsifiable: compare success when only the name changes against success when the layout changes.
- **Thai testbed:** OR instrument handover and teaching scrub technique to nursing students. The link to the Ronnapee lab and to KCMH's Physical-AI programme with True gives a partner path. Impact is medium; the frontier question is the main contribution.
- **Laptop experiment:** 2× SO-101 (leader–follower) + LeRobot; 200–400 teleoperated demonstrations over 6 instruments; SmolVLA-450M fine-tune (reportedly ~10–16 GB, second-hand figure) ~40–80 GPU-h; ACT as the no-language baseline; π0 inference-only as a reference.
- **Socratic test:** *The task actually requires language.* Month 1: with 60 demonstrations, train ACT (no language) and SmolVLA. **Kill** if ACT matches SmolVLA (there is no grounding question to answer). **Kill** if both are below 30% success (hardware becomes the thesis).

### 5. Can generative video models render triage cues? A plausibility audit for EMS training scenes
- **Pitch:** Thailand has 1,790 paramedics against a target of 7,500 and relies on 111,605 volunteers, so mass-casualty triage training has to scale. Generative video could produce endless drill scenes, but only if the *clinical cues* (breathing, bleeding, responsiveness, posture) actually render.
- **Paradigm & research question (video world models as training-data generators):** SurgVeo found a "plausibility gap" in surgery: the video looks real but the meaning is wrong. *Do open video generators that run on a 16 GB GPU render START/SALT triage cues well enough that blinded emergency physicians recover the intended triage category? Can a cue-conditioned fine-tune close the gap?* This is falsifiable: measure clinician agreement with the intended category, and check whether a VLM triage agent's accuracy on generated scenes predicts its accuracy on staged real drills.
- **Thai testbed:** EMS volunteer and paramedic training, plus flood and road mass-casualty drills (Hat Yai floods, November 2025). Footage is ground-level or pole-mounted, because CAAT rules (from 17 May 2026) make drone flights impractical; an aerial viewpoint is optional and rendered.
- **Laptop experiment:** a Wan-class 1.3B or LTX-class open video model (VRAM fit unverified; measure it in week 1). 200 generated casualty clips across 4 triage categories, rated by 2 blinded emergency physicians. Optionally a LoRA cue-conditioning fine-tune (~60–120 GPU-h). Real comparison: a staged drill with volunteer actors (needs IRB approval).
- **Socratic test:** *Clinical cues are renderable at all at this model size.* Month 1: 50 clips, 2 raters. If agreement with the intended category is below 50%, the *negative result* becomes the contribution (a triage plausibility benchmark) and the trainer pivots to Gaussian-splat captures of staged drills.

**If I were the student, I'd pick #1.** It uses public data from day 1, the transfer question is sharp and unanswered, and its Thai outcome is something a clinician on the committee can measure. **#2 is the bolder pick** if an emergency-medicine or point-of-care-ultrasound partner with a phantom is available. It is the only idea here that is *physical* (probe in hand), *frontier* (a world model used for planning) and *educational* at once. #3 is the safe continuation of the existing RSI work.
