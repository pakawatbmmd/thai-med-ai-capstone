# Discursive care AI (intake, triage, diagnosis, guidance, referral, follow-up through dialogue): frontier, regulation, Thailand, open problems (as of 21 Oct 2026)

Verification legend used throughout: **[P]** = read from primary source this session (paper abstract, regulator page, official news). **[S]** = secondary coverage only (news, blog, search-engine synthesis); check before citing in the thesis. **[C]** = company/self-reported claim, not independently verified. **(older)** = pre-2025.

## Q1. State of the art in AI-first clinical encounters: systems and evidence (2024–2026)

### Takeaway
The frontier has moved from simulated OSCEs to the first prospective real-patient studies and RCTs. In all of them the AI does intake or pre-visit reasoning with a human safety net, and none shows better patient outcomes yet. The strongest evidence types are: (1) Google AMIE's first prospective real-patient study (Lancet, Oct 2026; n≈100, supervised, zero safety stops, DDx quality matched PCPs); (2) Chinese RCTs of patient-facing pre-consultation LLMs (PreA, n=2,069, −28.7% consult time); (3) the Penda/OpenAI cluster RCT (clinician-facing, safe, no significant outcome gain). Headline accuracy claims such as MAI-DxO 85.5% and Doctronic 99.2% come from company benchmarks or concordance studies, not from outcomes.

### Cited Findings
**Google AMIE family**
- **AMIE prospective real-world study [P]:** prospective, single-arm feasibility study at an academic medical center's urgent primary-care appointments (BIDMC). 100 adult patients held a text-chat history-taking session with AMIE up to 5 days before the visit, under real-time human safety supervisors. AMIE's DDx contained the final diagnosis (chart review at 8 weeks) in 90% of cases, with top-3 accuracy 75%. Blinded DDx quality did not differ from PCPs (p=0.6), nor did management appropriateness or safety (p=0.1, p=1.0). PCPs beat AMIE on management **practicality (p=0.003)** and **cost-effectiveness (p=0.004)**. No consultation needed a safety stop under the prespecified criteria. Patient attitudes toward AI improved (p<0.001). — [arXiv 2603.08448](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08448); [Google Research blog](https://research.google/blog/exploring-the-feasibility-of-conversational-diagnostic-ai-in-a-real-world-clinical-study/)
- Publication details [S]: reported as published in *The Lancet* on 8 Oct 2026, with 114 enrolled and 98 completing. Supervisors noted one hallucination. Top-1 accuracy is reported as 56%. Physicians reviewed the AMIE transcript before 44 visits, found it helpful in 33, and said it might have changed their behaviour in 25. Sample-size figures conflict between sources (100 vs 98/114). — [NOPE Insights summary](https://insights.nope.net/2026-lancet-amie-conversational-diagnostic-ai-primary-care-feasibility); [R&D World](https://www.rdworldonline.com/google-amie-study-adds-evidence-of-medical-ais-promise-with-patients-under-doctors-supervision/)
- Companion Nature Medicine commentary by the AMIE authors [S]: argues that benchmarks cannot earn clinical trust and that trust has to come from prospective real-world studies. — [AI Weekly](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/amie-authors-benchmarks-cant-earn-medical-ai-trust-in-clinics)
- A registered AMIE urgent-care study exists: NCT06911398 [S]. — [ClinicalTrials.gov](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06911398)
- **g-AMIE, guardrailed with physician oversight (arXiv Jul 2025) [P/S]:** AMIE takes the history under guardrails that forbid individualized medical advice, then hands a draft assessment to a supervising PCP who stays accountable. This allows **asynchronous oversight**. In a randomized, blinded virtual OSCE with 60 scenarios, g-AMIE beat the comparison groups (nurse practitioners/PAs and PCPs under the same guardrails) on intake, summaries and proposed DDx/management. The authors say oversight was more time-efficient than standalone PCP consults, and caution that the text-only format likely underestimates clinicians. — [arXiv 2507.15743](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.15743); [Google blog](https://www.research.google/blog/enabling-physician-centered-oversight-for-amie/)
- **AMIE for longitudinal disease management (Nature, 2026) [S]:** a two-agent design (Dialogue agent plus Management-reasoning agent on long-context Gemini). Randomized blinded virtual OSCE: 21 PCPs, 100 multi-visit scenarios, each with 3 text visits. AMIE was non-inferior throughout and better at the first visit on plan appropriateness; the gap closed by later visits. All encounters were simulated. — [Nature s41586-026-10764-5](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10764-5); [arXiv 2503.06074](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.06074)
- (older, from training knowledge, not re-fetched) Original AMIE (text OSCE, 159 scenarios, 20 PCPs; AMIE better on 28/32 specialist axes), published in *Nature* 2025 as "Towards conversational diagnostic artificial intelligence." Treat it as the simulation-era baseline.

**Microsoft MAI-DxO**
- The Sequential Diagnosis Benchmark (SDBench) turns 304 NEJM CPC cases into stepwise encounters. A gatekeeper model reveals information only when asked, and every test has a virtual cost. MAI-DxO with o3 reached **80%** accuracy (85.5% in its max-accuracy configuration) against a **20%** average for 21 generalist physicians, at 20% lower cost than physicians and 70% lower than plain o3. The orchestration gains generalized across model families. **[C]**: self-reported, simulated costs, physicians barred from references and colleagues, rare-disease case mix. — [Microsoft AI blog](https://microsoft.ai/news/the-path-to-medical-superintelligence/); [MSR publication](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/sequential-diagnosis-with-language-models/)
- No 2026 prospective or real-patient MAI-DxO study turned up this session (see Gaps).

Medical records connect via b.well, plus Apple Health and other apps. OpenAI says health data is not used for training and is stored separately. Coverage notes that **HIPAA does not apply** to records a consumer moves into it, and the product is not offered in Europe. A lawsuit filed the day before launch seeks a pause (unverified). — [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/introducing-chatgpt-health/); [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/01/07/openai-chatgpt-health-medical-records.html); [TechTimes](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/321431/20260723/chatgpt-health-goes-nationwide-your-medical-records-lose-hipaa-protection.htm); [Paubox](https://www.paubox.com/blog/chatgpt-health-launches-nationwide-but-medical-records-not-hipaa-protected)
- **Claude for Healthcare [S]:** launched at JPM, Jan 2026, for providers and payers. A BAA/HIPAA-ready environment, with uses such as prior-auth review, patient-message triage and record summarization. Consumer connections to Apple Health and Android Health Connect are opt-in, and the healthcare workspace is not used for training. — [Fierce Healthcare](https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/ai-and-machine-learning/jpm26-anthropic-launches-claude-healthcare-targeting-health-systems-payers); [TechTarget](https://www.techtarget.com/healthtechanalytics/news/366637160/Claude-for-Healthcare-launches); [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2026/01/12/claude_anthropic_healthcare/)

**Digital-native "AI clinician" companies**
- **Doctronic [P/C]:** retrospective study of 500 consecutive urgent-care telehealth encounters. Top diagnosis matched the clinician in 81% of cases and the treatment plan in **99.2%**, with "no clinical hallucinations." In discordant cases, experts judged the AI superior in 36.1% and the human in 9.3%. **All authors hold Doctronic equity.** The study measures concordance, not correctness. — [medRxiv 2025.07.14.25331406](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.07.14.25331406.full.pdf); [arXiv 2507.22902](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.22902v1); [critique](https://www.onedayadvisor.com/2026/09/AI-doctor-Doctronic.html?m=0)
- **Counsel Health [C]:** $25M Series A (Oct 2025, a16z and GV) for a "physician-supervised AI front door." The AI handles conversation; Counsel physicians step in for diagnosis, prescriptions and referrals and follow up until resolution. Claims >100,000 members. — [BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20251016535098/en/Counsel-Health-Raises-$25M-to-Launch-Physician-Supervised-AI-Front-Door-for-Healthcare)
- **K Health / Cedars-Sinai CS Connect [P-abstract/S]:** retrospective single-center study (Annals of Internal Medicine, Apr 2025) of 461 virtual urgent-care visits comparing the initial AI recommendation with the final physician decision. Adjudicators rated them equal in ~68%, favoured the AI in 21% and the physician in 11%. K Health funded the study. Secondary sources quote "77% AI optimal vs 67% physicians," which does not match the adjudication breakdown. — [Annals](https://www.acpjournals.org/doi/10.7326/ANNALS-24-03283); [AHA](https://www.aha.org/role-hospitals-cedars-sinais-ai-tool-offers-247-patient-support-shows-promising-results)
- **Hippocratic AI [C]:** non-diagnostic voice agents for calls, follow-up and nurse co-pilot work. Claims >10M real patient calls with Polaris, a "99.9% clinical safety score," and 0.00% severe-harm events. Agents "do not diagnose or prescribe" and escalate to nurses. New 2026 products are AI Front Door and Nurse Co-Pilot. There is no independent verification. — [arXiv 2603.29893](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.29893); [Hippocratic AI](https://hippocraticai.com/why-hippocratic-ai/)
- **Babylon Health (cautionary case):** went from a valuation near $2B to Chapter 7 liquidation (2023), with UK assets sold to eMed. UK regulators and doctors questioned whether its symptom checker missed serious illness, and its NHS contracts eroded. — [Becker's](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/healthcare-information-technology/digital-health/how-babylon-health-went-from-2b-to-bankrupt/); [Computing](https://www.computing.co.uk/news/4121796/babylon-health-future-nhs-goes-administration) (older, 2023)

**RCTs and prospective trials of patient-facing or AI-first encounters**
- **PreA (Nature Medicine, 2025/26) [S]:** 3-arm RCT (PreA-only / PreA with staff help / usual care) with 2,069 patients and 111 specialists across 24 disciplines in western China. PreA ran on GPT-4o-mini without clinical fine-tuning, using prompting, agents and a knowledge graph, and was co-designed with local stakeholders. Physician consult time fell **28.7%** (4.41→3.14 min; 95% CI 22.7–34.8%). Physician-rated care coordination rose from 1.73 to 3.69, and patient-rated ease of communication from 3.44 to 3.99. PreA-only matched PreA with staff, which the authors read as evidence of autonomous operation. — [Nature Medicine s41591-025-04176-7](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-025-04176-7); [JMAI commentary](https://jmai.amegroups.org/article/view/11525/html)
- **Community-codesigned primary-care chatbot RCT (Nature Health, Jan 2026) [S]:** 2,113 participants across 11 Chinese provinces. The primary endpoint was health awareness, not a clinical outcome. — [Nature Health s44360-025-00021-w](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44360-025-00021-w)
- **Penda Health / OpenAI "AI Consult" cluster RCT (Nature Medicine, 2026) [S]:** 16 Kenyan primary-care clinics, nearly 10,000 encounters. GPT-4o checked clinical officers' notes in the background. Treatment failure fell 23%, which was not significant. The tool was safe, cost ~US$0.04 per patient, and clinicians rated it helpful. A co-author estimated ~139,000 patients would be needed to detect a meaningful outcome difference. — [Nature Medicine s41591-026-04503-6](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-026-04503-6); [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/07/23/g-s1-134929/ai-artificial-intelligence-healthcare)
- **Oxford randomized preregistered study (Nature Medicine, Feb 2026) [S + preprint]:** 1,298 UK adults, 10 scenarios each. LLMs alone identified a relevant condition in 94.7% (GPT-4o) to 99.2% (Llama 3) of cases. **Humans using the LLMs identified one in ≤34.5% of cases, worse than controls doing their own research (47%)**, and were no better at choosing a disposition. — [arXiv 2504.18919](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.18919); [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/ais-medical-skills-are-stuck-behind-a-human-bottleneck-according-to-new-oxford-research/)
- A registered RCT protocol for a structured, question-driven LLM interface to improve lay diagnosis and triage: NCT07728513 (no results yet). — [protocol PDF](https://cdn.clinicaltrials.gov/large-docs/13/NCT07728513/Prot_SAP_000.pdf)

### Inferences
- The field's ladder in 2026 runs: simulated OSCE (AMIE 2024–25, g-AMIE, longitudinal AMIE) → retrospective concordance (CS Connect, Doctronic) → supervised prospective feasibility (AMIE/BIDMC) → pragmatic RCT with process outcomes (PreA) → RCT with clinical outcomes (Penda: null and underpowered). **No study yet shows that an autonomous, unsupervised discursive-care AI improves patient outcomes.**
- The Oxford result and AMIE's practicality and cost deficit together locate the frontier's weakness in the **interaction** (what users say and leave out) and in **context-specific management** (what is practical and affordable here), not in medical knowledge. Both are local by nature, which favours a Thailand-specific contribution.
- PreA shows that a small, un-fine-tuned model with a co-designed agent scaffold produced RCT-grade process gains. For a laptop-scale capstone, the system design and evaluation protocol can be the contribution; a giant model is not needed.

### Gaps
- No 2026 real-patient or prospective MAI-DxO result was found.
- I could not open the full Lancet AMIE paper, so the top-1 accuracy, n=114/98 and hallucination details are secondary.
- Penda trial full statistics (CIs, the exact 14-day definition) were not retrieved (Nature paywall). An earlier Penda/OpenAI observational study (~40k visits) was not verified this session.
- K Health standalone outcomes, Ada Health's 2025–26 status, and the size and evidence of Counsel's deployment were not found.

## Q2. Performance-floor evidence: missed diagnoses, unsafe behaviour, incidents

### Takeaway
Floor failures in 2025–26 are mostly documented in benchmarks, not in incident registries. Models fail to ask before acting, fail to abstain, miss required escalations, and are miscalibrated. Real-world serious incidents are scarce: Utah reports none for Doctronic, and AMIE had zero safety stops. Those deployments are small and heavily supervised, though, so "no incidents" is weak evidence.

### Cited Findings
- **CARE-Bench (arXiv, Aug 2026) [P]:** per-turn patient-facing triage with 4 action labels. 500 cases, 1,059 disclosure prefixes, 11 models. Unprompted macro-F1 ran 31.2–50.4; prompted, 46.9–63.4. Models **recommend care before the needed clarification**. When the right move was to ask, **only 33.5%** of prompted outputs asked. The authors say triage is "not a simple prompting problem" and call for evaluating action timing before deployment. Code and data are released. — [arXiv 2608.03731](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03731)
- **Escalation-reliability audit (arXiv, Oct 2026) [P]:** 400 synthetic workflow episodes scored as ANSWER / ASK / ABSTAIN / ESCALATE, with a 5:1 cost for missed versus unnecessary escalation, across five open models of 1.7B–9.7B. Clean accuracy reached 0.97, but **uncertainty-prompted models missed 32–97% of required escalations**. The 9.7B model fell from 0.97 to 0.78 when evidence conflicted. Verbalized confidence had ECE 0.10–0.54 and failure AUROC 0.48–0.77, and a confidence-only policy gave no utility gain. A workflow-aware controller cut missed escalations to 0.12–0.24 at specificity 0.83–0.93. **Rule-based detectors went from recall 1.00 on trigger phrases to 0.00 on unseen paraphrases.** Labels were single-author, not clinician-adjudicated. — [arXiv 2610.21270](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.21270)
- **MedAbstain (Jan 2026) [S]:** high-accuracy models often fail to abstain when uncertain. Explicit abstention options help more than scale or prompting. — [arXiv 2601.12471](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.12471)
- **"LLMs (Almost) Never Abstain Under Medical Uncertainty" (ACL 2026) [S]:** removes the gold answer from MCQA and adds an abstain option; models rarely abstain. — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.1365/)
- **Multi-turn diagnosis "Hold, Lure, Self-Correction" benchmark (Apr 2026) [S, title only]:** tests whether models hold a premature diagnosis or self-correct as information arrives. — [arXiv 2604.04325](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.04325)
- MediQ (2024, older): prompting LLMs to ask questions *degraded* performance, an early sign that information gathering is a capability gap. — [arXiv 2406.00922](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.00922)
- Non-LLM baselines can beat LLMs at triage. In a 2026 mixed-methods study (Serbia, 156 vignette interactions), a rules-only triage chatbot scored ~85% adjusted accuracy versus ~35–49% for LLM-enhanced versions. The sample was small. — [Stankić et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1177/14604582261485150)
- **Utah/Doctronic real-world floor [P]:** "no serious safety incidents" reported to the state. Disagreements between the AI and supervising clinicians were rated no, minor, or minor-to-moderate risk. A third-party red-team found vulnerabilities under adversarial use. The Office "lacks robust benefit data." — [Utah OAIP Doctronic page](https://commerce.utah.gov/ai/regulatory-relief-4/authorized-pilots/doctronic/)
- AMIE/BIDMC had zero safety stops with real-time supervisors, plus one hallucination noted [S]. — [arXiv 2603.08448](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08448); [NOPE Insights](https://insights.nope.net/2026-lancet-amie-conversational-diagnostic-ai-primary-care-feasibility)
- Pennsylvania sued Character.AI (May 2026) over a chatbot allegedly posing as a doctor [S]. — [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/05/05/nx-s1-5812861/characterai-chatbot-medical-advice-pennsylvania-lawsuit)
- Prior project finding (Thai): the student's own landscape report found ThaiLLM-27B-Prescreen with **F1 = 0 for "Observe at Home"** and no independent Thai benchmark of any Thai medical LLM. — [project README](/home/user/thai-med-ai-capstone/README.md); [report](/home/user/thai-med-ai-capstone/reports/Thai%20medical%20LLM%20landscape.md)

### Inferences
- The best-documented failure mode is **acting too early**: answering or recommending care before asking, and missing escalations. That is a sequential-decision problem, which suits an agent or controller contribution better than a better-QA-model contribution.
- **Direct risk to the student's current plan:** the Oct 2026 escalation audit shows that keyword or rule detectors collapse on paraphrase (recall 1.00→0.00). If Part 2 uses rule-based verifiers as RL reward, reward hacking may be less of a risk than verifier blindness (false negatives on paraphrased Thai or dialect red flags). That could itself become the research question. Pairing a rule verifier with paraphrase and dialect stress tests would be a natural extension.

### Gaps
- No public adverse-event registry for discursive AI was found, in the US or Thailand.
- No study was found that measures missed-diagnosis rates of autonomous AI encounters with real follow-up at scale.

## Q3. Regulation of AI acting without a physician; billing codes (US, UK, EU, Thailand)

### Takeaway
Only one jurisdiction lets AI make an autonomous clinical decision, and it does so through a sandbox: Utah's Doctronic pilot for prescription *renewals*. The trend in other US states runs the opposite way, banning AI from presenting as a clinician, especially in mental health. FDA has authorized no generative-AI device. The EU delayed AI Act obligations for high-risk medical AI to 2027–28. CPT has an AI taxonomy (assistive, augmentative, autonomous) but no code for an AI-performed E&M/discursive visit.

### Cited Findings
**Utah sandbox: Doctronic Regulatory Mitigation Agreement [P]**
- Runs Oct 2025–Oct 2026 with an optional one-year renewal (public launch Jan 2026). The AI **renews existing** prescriptions for 30, 60 or 90 days only: no new prescriptions, no dose changes, no controlled substances. Butalbital and flecainide were removed from the formulary, and formularies across companies range from ~20 to ~200 drugs.
- Safeguards: identity verification, a named physician on each renewal, auto-escalation on complexity or conflicting information, limited refills without a follow-up visit, a pharmacist hotline, and malpractice cover for AI liability.
- Phases: in **Phase 1** a physician reviews *every* renewal. **Phase 2** (direct to pharmacist) unlocks per medication group after 250 fills in that group, with OAIP approval. Doctronic files monthly reports, and a public report was issued 19 May 2026.
- Sources: [Utah OAIP](https://commerce.utah.gov/ai/regulatory-relief-4/authorized-pilots/doctronic/); [Utah news release](https://commerce.utah.gov/2026/01/06/news-release-utah-and-doctronic-announce-groundbreaking-partnership-for-ai-prescription-medication-renewals/)
- Waivers cover licensure, scope of practice, professional conduct and telehealth-prescribing rules [S]. — [Fierce Healthcare](https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/ai-and-machine-learning/doctronic-partners-utah-ai-sandbox); [Deseret News](https://www.deseret.com/business/2026/01/06/utah-artificial-intelligence-prescription-renewal-regulatory-sandbox-innovation-patient-safety/)
- Utah's medical licensing board called in Apr 2026 for a halt, and the state refused. The AMA has raised concerns [S]. — [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/utah-ai-prescription-refills-doctronic)

**Other US states, 2025–2026 [S]**
- **California AB 489** (effective 1 Jan 2026) bars AI from using titles such as "doctor" or "nurse" in a way that implies a licensed human. Each use is a separate violation, enforced by licensing boards.
- **Illinois WOPR Act** bans AI therapy but allows administrative and supplementary AI under a licensed clinician.
- 2026 laws: **Tennessee SB 1580** (AI may not be represented as a mental-health professional); **Delaware HB 191** (AI may not be licensed as, or present as, a physician, PA or nurse); **Missouri SB 1019** (effective 28 Aug 2026); and Idaho and Nebraska Conversational AI Safety Acts.
- Sources conflict in places, so verify the statutes. — [Holland & Knight, May 2026](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/05/states-continue-efforts-to-regulate-ai-in-healthcare); [Becker's](https://www.beckersbehavioralhealth.com/ai-2/5-states-restrict-ai-therapy-chatbots-in-2026/); [MultiState](https://www.multistate.ai/updates/vol-95-ai-professional-licensing-laws-state-regulation)

**FDA [P/S]**
- The Digital Health Advisory Committee met on 6 Nov 2025 on generative-AI mental-health devices (case: a prescription LLM chatbot for MDD). Panelists favoured a total-product-lifecycle approach, with defined serious adverse events, a **mandatory escalation path to a human clinician**, and post-market monitoring.
- **No generative-AI device is authorized** for clinical use. The PCCP final guidance (Aug 2025) governs pre-specified model changes.
- Sources: [FDA 24-hour summary](https://www.fda.gov/media/189618/download); [STAT](https://www.statnews.com/2025/11/05/fda-digital-advisers-therapy-chatbots-regulating-generative-ai/); [Hogan Lovells](https://www.hlc.com/en/publications/fdas-digital-health-advisory-committee-weighs-guardrails-for-generative-ai-in-mental-health-devices)

**Billing [P/S]**
- CPT Appendix S defines **assistive** (detects data, no conclusion), **augmentative** (analyzes or quantifies, clinician interprets) and **autonomous** (interprets and concludes with minimal or no clinician). It was revised Mar 2026, and the CPT Panel accepted further clarifications in May 2026.
- 2026 CPT added seven Category III AI codes, all measurement or imaging (e.g., ECG 0902T/0932T, chest CT 0877T–0880T, prostate biopsy 0898T). **None covers a conversational triage or E&M-type AI encounter.**
- Sources: [AMA Appendix S](https://www.ama-assn.org/practice-management/cpt/cpt-appendix-s-taxonomy-artificial-intelligence-medical-services-procedures); [AMA](https://www.ama-assn.org/practice-management/cpt/cpt-codes-offer-language-report-ai-enabled-health-services); [Discoveries in Health Policy critique](https://www.discoveriesinhealthpolicy.com/2026/06/two-ais-review-amas-new-artificial.html)

**EU and UK [S]**
- Digital Omnibus on AI, Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, in force 27 Jul 2026: high-risk obligations for AI in Annex I products (incl. medical devices) move to **2 Aug 2028**, and stand-alone Annex III systems to 2 Dec 2027. Medical-device interplay (MDR) is still in flux. — [Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/); [Cooley](https://cdp.cooley.com/digital-ai-omnibus-delays-key-deadlines-introduces-new-rules/)
- MHRA AI Airlock: Phase 2 had 7 products, incl. the TORTUS clinical assistant and the Numan conversational and monitoring system, and reportedly ended in 2026. Phase 3 is in design, with £3.6M over three years. Selection is not approval. — [Fieldfisher](https://www.fieldfisher.com/en/insights/mhra-ai-airlock-from-pilot-sandbox-to-scaling-regulatory-pathway-for-ai-medical-devices); [Healthcare Management UK](https://www.healthcare-management.uk/tools-selected-phase-mhras-ai-airlock-programme)

**Thailand [P-news/S]**
- No Thai law or Medical Council (แพทยสภา) rule specifically permits or bans AI diagnosis was found.
- The Medical Council secretary (Aug and Oct 2026) says AI cannot replace physician judgement and accountability. The Council will set standards and, per its Oct 2026 term report, is drafting new law; it is unclear whether that covers AI.
- Telemedicine is governed by Medical Council Notification 54/2563 (2020).
- Sources: [Hfocus Aug 2026](https://www.hfocus.org/content/2026/08/38956); [Hfocus Oct 2026](https://www.hfocus.org/content/2026/10/39882); [Hfocus Oct 2026 term report](https://www.hfocus.org/content/2026/10/39901); [ABeam telehealth paper](https://www.abeam.com/content/dam/abeam/th/en/insights/013/wp_013.pdf)
- **Thai professional-boundary precedent:** the Medical Council sued NHSO and the Pharmacy Council over pharmacists treating 32 minor-ailment groups under UC. The Supreme Administrative Court accepted the case, and NHSO says the service continues pending a ruling. — [Isranews](https://www.isranews.org/article/isranews/133298-isra-28.html); [Isranews](https://www.isranews.org/article/isranews-news/133335-isra-280.html)

### Inferences
- The global regulatory frontier is **narrow, protocolized autonomy** (renewals; no new drugs, no dose changes) with a phased step-down of human review triggered by volume (250 fills per group). That is a concrete template for a "performance-floor certification" protocol a capstone could formalize and simulate for Thailand.
- Utah's medical board versus state, and Thailand's Medical Council versus NHSO and pharmacists, are the same fight over scope of practice. In Thailand, any AI-first discursive care will most plausibly enter as **decision support for pharmacists, อสม. or nurses** inside existing NHSO channels, not as an autonomous "AI doctor."

### Gaps
- The Utah 19 May 2026 public report was not read in full, and no Phase 2 transition has been confirmed.
- No Thai FDA (อย.) SaMD guidance specific to LLMs was found this session.
- Whether any Thai draft AI law (ETDA/DGA) covers clinical AI is unverified.
- No ICD/CPT code for AI triage or E&M was found; it was not determined whether the AMA has a pending application.

## Q4. Thailand: equivalent landscape and access gaps

### Takeaway
Thailand already has the institutional rails for discursive care: UC "30 baht anywhere" innovation units (pharmacies treating 32 symptom groups, private clinics, telemedicine at every รพ.สต.), NHSO and MOPH LINE OAs, the Mor Prom super-app, and ~1.07M อสม. on Smart อสม. What it lacks is a validated conversational triage or intake layer. Clinical-dialogue AI exists only as unvalidated hospital kiosks (Agnos at Bangkok Hospital), a COVID-era rule chatbot, and ministerial anecdotes of "AI-assisted" pharmacy dispensing. No evaluated Thai AI symptom-checker or triage deployment was found.

### Inferences
- **The Thai analogue of McKinsey's "visits diverted to AI" already exists, but with humans:** pharmacists handling 32 symptom groups (~8M consults), อสม., and รพ.สต. telemedicine (1.5M sessions). The AI insertion point with the highest leverage and lowest regulatory friction is a **pre-encounter conversational intake and triage agent that routes patients among home care, pharmacy, รพ.สต. telemedicine, hospital and 1669**, inside the LINE OA or Mor Prom channels that already exist.
- **Routing is a binding constraint in Thailand that the US literature lacks:** weekly NHSO quotas make "which channel, this week" a live decision. A triage agent that is quota-aware and knows UC entitlements is novel relative to AMIE, MAI-DxO and Doctronic.
- AMIE's weakness on "management practicality and cost" is exactly where Thai context matters: UC benefit package, the national essential drug list (บัญชียาหลัก), the 32-symptom pharmacy formulary, and distance to a hospital.
- The Thai data gap matches the McKinsey claim that "data availability, not model capability" is the limit. The NHSO LINE OA health-data pilot (diabetes) is the first consumer-facing UC data feed, which is relevant to progressive disclosure.

### Gaps
- No 2026 figure was found for pharmacy minor-ailment volume. The 6,000 pharmacies and ~8M consults are a May 2025 ministerial claim.
- No current OPD waiting-time statistics for MOPH hospitals were found.
- No usage numbers were found for the NHSO/MOPH LINE OAs or Mor Prom AI features.
- No AI features in Smart อสม. were found.
- No independent evaluation of any Thai AI symptom checker or triage system (Agnos, the MorDee triage, the pharmacy "AI") was found.
- The status of the Medical Council v. NHSO case in 2026 is unconfirmed.
- No Thai survey analogous to KFF's (share of Thais using chatbots for health) was found.

## Q5. Unsolved technical problems that could host a groundbreaking contribution (named open in 2025–2026)

### Takeaway
Most of the problems in the brief are explicitly named as open in 2025–26 work: question-asking and timing, escalation, calibration, practical and cost-aware management, and the human–AI interaction gap. Longitudinal follow-up is open in the real world (AMIE's is simulated only). Progressive disclosure and performance-floor certification are named by regulators and McKinsey but have essentially no technical literature. Dialect-robust medical triage has no benchmark at all. The weakest-covered, most Thai-specific intersection is **dialect- and paraphrase-robust escalation under information-gathering**, evaluated per turn.

### Cited Findings
- **Information gathering and action timing (open):** CARE-Bench: "not a simple prompting problem," only 33.5% preserve the ask-step, and premature care recommendations. — [arXiv 2608.03731](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03731). Earlier: MediQ found question-asking degraded performance. — [arXiv 2406.00922](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.00922)
- **Knowing when to escalate (open):** 32–97% of required escalations are missed; routing controls "need dedicated generalization tests"; rule detectors fail on paraphrase. — [arXiv 2610.21270](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.21270). The FDA advisory committee wants a mandatory escalation path to a human. — [FDA summary](https://www.fda.gov/media/189618/download)
- **Calibration and abstention in dialogue (open):** ECE 0.10–0.54 for verbalized confidence. — [arXiv 2610.21270](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.21270). Models rarely abstain. — [ACL 2026](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.1365/); [arXiv 2601.12471](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.12471)
- **Human–AI interaction gap (open):** LLM-alone 94.7–99.2% vs human+LLM ≤34.5%; the authors conclude that benchmarks and simulations do not predict real use. — [arXiv 2504.18919](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.18919)
- **Practical, cost-aware management (open):** AMIE lost to PCPs on practicality (p=0.003) and cost-effectiveness (p=0.004) with real patients. — [arXiv 2603.08448](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08448). MAI-DxO's cost-aware orchestration exists, but only on simulated US costs. — [Microsoft](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/sequential-diagnosis-with-language-models/)
- **Longitudinal follow-up agents (partly open):** AMIE multi-visit management is non-inferior in *simulated* 3-visit OSCEs, and the authors say real-world validation is required. — [Nature 2026](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10764-5). Hippocratic AI's follow-up calls are deployed at scale but non-diagnostic and self-evaluated. — [arXiv 2603.29893](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.29893)
- **Patient-pressure sycophancy:** MedPRESS (arXiv 2608.02520) was seen only as a citation and is unverified. — [arXiv listing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02520)
- **Performance-floor certification (named, no method):** Utah's approach is operational, not statistical: physician review of 100% of renewals until 250 fills per medication group, then step-down, monthly reporting, plus a red-team. The Office still "lacks robust benefit data." — [Utah OAIP](https://commerce.utah.gov/ai/regulatory-relief-4/authorized-pilots/doctronic/). The FDA panel favours a lifecycle approach with defined serious adverse events. — [STAT](https://www.statnews.com/2025/11/05/fda-digital-advisers-therapy-chatbots-regulating-generative-ai/). Penda shows outcome-powered RCTs would need ~139k patients. — [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/07/23/g-s1-134929/ai-artificial-intelligence-healthcare)
- **Multilingual and dialect (open, Thai-specific):** Isan ASR now exists. — [SCBX](https://www.scbx.com/en/news/scb-10x-unveils-typhoon-isan/). Dialect LLM quality varies widely. — [arXiv 2504.05898](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.05898). No medical-dialect triage benchmark was found.
- **Empathy evaluation:** AMIE work rates empathy through patient-actor and specialist axes; with real patients, favourable ratings ranged 48–96% across 16 criteria [S]. — [NOPE Insights](https://insights.nope.net/2026-lancet-amie-conversational-diagnostic-ai-primary-care-feasibility). No standardized, validated empathy metric for Thai clinical dialogue was found.
- **Progressive disclosure:** framed as a prerequisite by McKinsey (see the guidance summary). Products implement opt-in consent: ChatGPT Health and Claude choose what to share. — [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/introducing-chatgpt-health/); [TechTarget](https://www.techtarget.com/healthtechanalytics/news/366637160/Claude-for-Healthcare-launches). **No technical paper was found that formalizes how much patient data an agent should request, and when, against diagnostic value.**
- **Care-cascade avoidance:** named by McKinsey. MAI-DxO's per-test cost is the nearest technical proxy. — [Microsoft AI](https://microsoft.ai/news/the-path-to-medical-superintelligence/). No dialogue-agent paper on cascade avoidance was found.

### Inferences
Candidate capstone directions, ranked by novelty × Thai impact × laptop feasibility. These are my synthesis, not sourced claims.
1. **Thai CARE-Bench-style per-turn triage-and-escalation benchmark with dialect and paraphrase stress.**
   - Disclosure prefixes in Central, Isan, Northern and Southern Thai.
   - Actions: ask / home care / pharmacy-32 / รพ.สต. telemed / hospital / 1669.
   - Measures missed escalation at a 5:1 cost, ask-preservation rate and calibration.
   - No equivalent was found, and it extends the student's "Option B" audit directly.
2. **Escalation controller trained by self-play (connects to the RSI plan).** Instead of the LLM deciding alone, train a small workflow-state controller or verifier pair (rule plus learned). Measure when RL against verifier A hacks verifier B, with **paraphrase and dialect robustness as the held-out axis**. The Oct 2026 audit's rule-detector collapse (1.00→0.00) makes this timely.
3. **Value-of-information question policy (progressive disclosure formalized).** An agent that picks the next question or data request by expected reduction in triage risk per unit of patient burden or privacy. It would be evaluated on how often it asks before acting (CARE-Bench's 33.5% gap). No prior work formalizing progressive disclosure was found.
4. **Thai-system-aware management (fixing AMIE's practicality and cost gap).** Ground disposition and management in the UC benefit set, the 32-symptom pharmacy formulary and weekly quota and distance. Score "practicality" with Thai clinicians.
5. **Utah-style phased certification protocol for Thailand.** Statistically specify a performance floor: sequential testing to step down human review per symptom group, analogous to Utah's 250-fill gate. Simulate it on the benchmark. This is a methods contribution that regulators (อย., แพทยสภา, NHSO) could adopt.

Options 1 and 2 combine naturally into the "audit-first then RSI" plan in the README, with a stronger frontier hook: per-turn action timing plus paraphrase-robust verification.

### Gaps
- MedPRESS, Clindet-bench (arXiv 2602.22771), the "Hold, Lure, Self-Correction" benchmark and the Scientific Reports 2026 diagnostic-questioning-efficiency paper were seen only as titles or citations; contents unverified.
- CARE-Bench's language coverage and label names could not be confirmed from the abstract.
- No 2025–26 paper was found on longitudinal AI follow-up with *real* patients and outcomes, on cascade-aware dialogue agents, or on progressive-disclosure policies.
- Novelty of a Thai or dialect triage benchmark: a targeted search of ThaiJO and Thai-language theses was not done this session and should precede a proposal.
