## Notes on Evidence Quality & Ecosystem Gaps (As of October 2026)

* **Heavy Reliance on Translated or English Benchmarks over Native Thai Clinical Exams:** The strongest technical papers in the Thai ecosystem—such as SCB 10X & Siriraj's `Typhoon-Si-Med-Thinking-4B` ([arXiv:2509.20866](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.20866)) and `Eir-8B` ([arXiv:2409.08523](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.08523))—primarily report standardized scores on English benchmarks (`MedQA`, `MedMCQA`, `PubMedQA`, `MMLU-Pro Health`). While peer-reviewed evaluation on **Thailand's National Medical Licensing Examination (ThaiNLE Step 1)** exists for foreign frontier models (`GPT-4o` at 88.9%, `Claude 3.5 Sonnet` at 80.1%, `Gemini 1.5 Pro` at 72.8% in [JEEHP 2025;22:16](https://doi.org/10.3352/jeehp.2025.22.16)), domestic open-weight medical models have not published standardized, head-to-head scores on the same 304-question ThaiNLE dataset.
* **Scarcity of Peer-Reviewed Prospective Clinical Trials:** Among deployed conversational/LLM health tools in Thailand, only **DMIND** has published a peer-reviewed clinical concordance study against a gold-standard psychiatric scale (Thai HDRS-17, reporting 87.30% sensitivity and 59.50% specificity in [Chulalongkorn Med J](https://doi.org/10.56808/2673-060X.5495)). Commercial leaders like **CARIVA (`PreceptorAI`, `MOR-ASR`)** and **Agnos Health** report impressive operational metrics (>300,000 cases, 50% scribe time savings, 59% OPD wait-time reduction) in company press releases and hospital case studies, and CARIVA publishes strong sub-task papers at ACL/EMNLP/NeurIPS, but neither has published an independent clinical trial measuring diagnostic safety or patient outcomes in live hospital care.
* **Regulatory Positioning (Thai FDA SaMD vs. "Clinician-in-the-Loop" / Wellness Exemption):** While the Thai FDA (อย.), NECTEC (via its SQUAT testing lab), and TCELS actively enforce **Software as a Medical Device (SaMD)** and **IEC 62304** guidelines for computer-vision radiology AI (e.g., Perceptra's *Inspectra CXR/MMG* and Ramathibodi's *RAMAAI CXR* on the NSTDA Medical AI Data Platform) ([moph.go.th](https://www.moph.go.th/), [nectec.or.th](https://www.nectec.or.th/)), virtually all LLM-based deployments in Thailand currently avoid Class II/III SaMD registration by positioning themselves either as (1) **human-in-the-loop administrative/clinical co-pilots** (`PreceptorAI`, `MOR-ASR`, `Pharmate AI`, `Typhoon-Si-Med-Thinking-4B`) or (2) **preliminary non-diagnostic triage / administrative navigators** (`Agnos`, `Doctor at Home`, `The Medicative`, `Ooca mooca`, `SiData+ Admin Chatbot`).
* **Shift Toward Small, Sovereign, On-Premise Models (2025–2026):** Due to strict enforcement of Thailand's **Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA)** on sensitive health data, hospital deployments between 2025 and October 2026 have pivoted strongly toward compact (4B–8B parameter) open-weight models (`Typhoon-Si-Med-Thinking-4B`, `ThaiLLM-8B-MedApp`, `ThaiLLM-8B-SFT-IQ`, `pai1-medgemma-4b`, `Llama-3.1-EIRAI-8B`) that can run on local hospital GPUs with citation-grounded RAG rather than sending patient records to external cloud APIs.
