# Medical Drones (UAVs) in Healthcare, Emergency Medicine and Medical Education, with a Thailand Focus (as of 21 Oct 2026)

Scope note: sources are mainly 2024–2026. Older items are marked **[older]**. "Verified" means I read the primary or official page. "Snippet only" means the claim comes from a search summary or a secondary report I did not open in full. Key verification gaps are listed under each question.

## Q1. Global deployments and evidence: delivery (Zipline, Wing/Apian, Matternet, Swoop Aero), AED drones, blood/lab samples, SAR, mass-casualty triage (DARPA Triage Challenge), rPPG from the air

### Takeaway
Drone **logistics** for medicines, blood and lab samples is the only medical-drone use that runs at national scale. Zipline in Rwanda is the clearest case, but most outcome claims for it come from the company. **AED drones** have been shown feasible and safe in real cardiac arrests in Sweden and Denmark, but survival benefit is unproven and response-time gains depend on where drones are based. **Aerial triage and remote vital signs** are at the research and competition stage: DARPA's Triage Challenge finals run in Nov 2026, and the published drone-vital-sign studies used about 15–37 healthy participants.

### Cited Findings
**Logistics: Zipline**
- Zipline handles nearly all of Rwanda's pharmaceutical supply and delivery, with a drone dispatched on average about every 50 seconds (NPR, Aug 2026). The same reporting stresses that drones "can't do it all". — [NPR 2026-08-04](https://www.npr.org/2026/08/04/g-s1-136906/drones-healthcare-trump-administration-rwanda)
- The U.S. State Department agreed to provide up to **$150M** to Zipline on a pay-for-performance basis, with African governments paying service fees. The stated target is up to 15,000 health facilities across five countries. — [State Dept briefing](https://www.state.gov/digital-press-briefing-on-zipline-drone-delivery-and-the-america-first-global-health-strategy); [Digital Health Africa](https://digitalhealth-africa.org/u-s-announces-150m-partnership-to-expand-ziplines-medical-drone-deliveries-across-africa/)
- Governments reportedly cover about 73–80% of an estimated ~$400M three-year programme cost. — [Ecofin Agency](https://www.ecofinagency.com/news-infrastructures/0312-51064-zipline-pilot-program-signals-shift-in-africa-s-medical-logistics-model)
- In Feb 2026 Rwanda signed the first expansion under the U.S. award, including an urban network in Kigali, described as Africa's first. — [Zipline newsroom](https://www.zipline.com/newsroom/rwanda-expands-with-zipline-to-become-first-country-in-the-world-with-nationwide-autonomous-delivery-including-africa-s-first-urban-drone-delivery-network)
- Côte d'Ivoire: Zipline reports >3.55M medical products delivered since its 2023 pilot, including ~20,500 blood-product units. The network is expanding to 10 hubs. Nigeria is planned to grow from 3 to 15 hubs by 2028. These are company and secondary figures. — [AU-Startups](https://au-startups.com/news/cote-divoire-zipline-health-logistics-expansion)
- Zipline's claim of a "51% reduction in maternal deaths" comes from company materials. An independent analysis says the independent evidence is thinner than the expansion narrative suggests (**unverified / contested**). — [APPI News](https://en.appi.news/articles/rwanda-drone-healthcare-delivery-limits/)
- Zipline's funding (>$600M raised Jan 2026 at $7.6B valuation; reported talks of ~$1B at ~$20B in Sep 2026) is secondary reporting and **unverified**. — [AU-Startups](https://au-startups.com/news/cote-divoire-zipline-health-logistics-expansion); [note.com (citing Bloomberg)](https://note.com/bon0404/n/ne63111caf94b?hl=en)

**Logistics: Wing/Apian (UK NHS), Swoop Aero, Matternet**
- Guy's and St Thomas' NHS Foundation Trust ran a six-month trial with Apian and Wing, flying urgent blood samples between Guy's and St Thomas' hospitals. The trips took under 2 minutes by drone versus >30 minutes by road. The UK CAA granted airspace approval and NATS handled integration with crewed aircraft. Announced Sep 2024 **[older, 2024]**. — [GSTT news](https://www.guysandstthomas.nhs.uk/news/nhs-blood-samples-be-delivered-drones-london-first); [Wing](https://wing.com/news/NHS-drone-delivery); [Digital Health Nov 2024](https://www.digitalhealth.net/2024/11/drones-deliver-urgent-blood-samples-for-guys-and-st-thomas)
- One report puts the scale at up to 10 deliveries per day, each carrying 20 samples (snippet only). — [Air Med & Rescue](https://www.airmedandrescue.com/latest/news/london-hospitals-trial-drones-blood-sample-delivery)
- **Swoop Aero** (Australia), a medical-logistics pioneer known for Africa and Pacific vaccine deliveries, entered voluntary administration on 14 Oct 2024 and was later liquidated. It reported a FY2023 loss of $9.3M on $6.6M revenue. This shows how fragile the sector's business model is. — [Startup Daily](https://www.startupdaily.net/topic/business/drone-delivery-startup-swoop-aero-stalls-lands-in-administration/); [Startup Daily (liquidation)](https://www.startupdaily.net/topic/business/drone-delivery-startup-swoop-aero-lands-in-liquidation/); [Capital Brief](https://www.capitalbrief.com/article/ambitious-drone-startup-swoop-aero-crashes-into-liquidation-f3979228-a5b6-4754-aab6-62a66c2ba044/)
- Blood samples carried by drone showed no haemolysis in a feasibility study. — [PMC12183833](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12183833/); earlier lab work: [Ling et al., Transfusion 2019 **[older]**](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/trf.15195)

**AED drones (out-of-hospital cardiac arrest, OHCA)**
- Sweden (Everdrone, Gothenburg; Karolinska Institutet), Apr 2021–May 2022, published Lancet Digital Health Nov 2023 **[older]**:
  - Drones beat ambulances in about 67–70% of cases where they delivered, by a median of about 3 min 14 s.
  - In the 18 real cardiac arrests, the caller used the AED in 6 (33%). A shock was advised in 2, and 1 patient survived.
  - Reported limits: restricted air-traffic-control hours, delays in controlled airspace, weather limits, and the low incidence of OHCA.
  - — [Lancet Digital Health](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/landig/article/PIIS2589-7500(23)00217-0/fulltext); [KI news](https://news.ki.se/drones-enabled-the-use-of-defibrillators-before-ambulance-arrival)
  - Counts conflict across sources: press coverage says 55 deliveries, while a Lancet commentary says 72 deployments out of 211 alerts. I could not reconcile them.
- Denmark: a semi-autonomous drone delivered AEDs to real OHCAs (Resuscitation 2025). Only 33% (16/49) of suspected OHCAs during operating hours were eligible for take-off, and 13/16 were confirmed arrests. Time gains were limited because ambulances were already fast. — [Resuscitation 2025](https://www.resuscitationjournal.com/article/S0300-9572(25)00056-5/fulltext)
- A scoping review (Resusc Plus 2025) concludes the evidence supports safety and feasibility. It found one case report of a drone-delivered AED shocking a patient before EMS arrived. Modelling (North Carolina) projected survival in witnessed arrests rising from 14.5% to 19.4% with drones plus first-responder AEDs. — [ScienceDirect scoping review](https://sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2666520424002923)
- A UK simulation (Smith et al., Resusc Plus 2025) delivered the AED in 9 of 11 single-bystander simulations. The main delays were on scene, after the drone arrived, so bystanders and call-handlers need more support. — [PMC12355114](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12355114/)
- A qualitative study of 14 OHCA bystanders (Dec 2025) found they were broadly accepting of drone-delivered AEDs. — [PMC12674532](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12674532)
- Registered trials: [NCT06334718 "3D Project"](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06334718); [NCT04723368](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04723368) (status not checked).
- Placement optimisation is an active modelling topic, e.g. a Bayesian learning approach to drone coverage for cardiac arrest in Scotland (arXiv 2603.23134, 2026). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.23134)

**Mass-casualty triage from drones: DARPA Triage Challenge (DTC)**
- Goal: find injury signatures for "scalable, timely, and accurate triage in mass casualty events". The Data competition uses more than 6,000 trauma cases. PM: Jeremy Pamplin, MD (DARPA BTO). — [DARPA programme summary PDF (May 2026)](https://www.darpa.mil/sites/default/files/attachment/2026-05/darpa-triage-challenge-program-summary.pdf)
- Event 1 (Sep 2024, Guardian Centers, Perry GA). Systems: DART 92.5, Coordinated Robotics 84.0, RoboScout 73.0. Data: Coordinated Robotics 625, MSAI 555, CAMA 532. Virtual: Coordinated Robotics 369.2. — [DARPA events page](https://www.darpa.mil/research/challenges/darpa-triage-challenge/events)
- Event 2 (Systems 26 Sep–3 Oct 2025, Guardian Centers):
  - Courses were a C-130 crash and a night ambush, with smoke, darkness and obstructions.
  - Systems leaderboard: DART (Battelle, DARPA-funded) 1st; RoboScout 2nd; Coordinated Robotics 3rd (top self-funded, $300k); UAS-DTU ($150k).
  - Data leaderboard: MSAI 1st (DARPA-funded); Coordinated Robotics 1st prize ($300k).
  - — [DARPA news Nov 2025](https://www.darpa.mil/news/2025/dart-msai-triumph-darpa-triage-challenge); [events page](https://www.darpa.mil/research/challenges/darpa-triage-challenge/events)
- Event 2 Data scoring had three runs: R1 expanded EHR, R2 basic EHR, and **R3 vitals only**. — [DARPA events page](https://www.darpa.mil/research/challenges/darpa-triage-challenge/events)
- The Event 2 systems scenario had an optional "Accurate Vitals" gate. Its tolerances are reportedly HR ±3 bpm and RR ±5 breaths/min, measured with stand-off RGB (colour-change HR), IR and radar sensors. This is from search summaries of DARPA material; I did not confirm it in the primary text. — [DARPA DTC summary](https://www.darpa.mil/sites/default/files/attachment/2026-05/darpa-triage-challenge-program-summary.pdf); [UASweekly Dec 2025](https://uasweekly.com/2025/12/10/battelles-dart-drone-triage-system-wins-second-darpa-challenge/)
- **Finals (Event 3, 2026), Disaster City, College Station, Texas:** Data competition 10 Oct 2026; Systems 5–13 Nov 2026; awards 13 Nov 2026. **Results were not available as of 21 Oct 2026.** — [DARPA events page](https://www.darpa.mil/research/challenges/darpa-triage-challenge/events)

**Remote vital signs from drones (aerial rPPG / thermal)**
- Tayfur et al., Sci Rep, 10 Mar 2026 (Türkiye and Belmont Univ., USA):
  - Methods: HR from mean frame colour in RGB video plus signal processing; RR from thermal changes in the nasal region; temperature from the forehead thermal maximum; SpO₂ from a deep model on thermal features.
  - Sample: n = 37 healthy-ish adults (mean age 29.7), 30 indoor and 7 outdoor recordings, compared with a bedside monitor.
  - "Accuracy" indoor: SpO₂ 98.65%, temp 98.59%, HR 97.70%, RR 85.22%. Outdoor: 99.60%, 98.48%, 96.85%, 82.83%.
  - Mean HR bias −0.31 bpm indoors. The accuracy metric is not clearly defined in the abstract, and RR is the weakest measure.
  - — [PubMed 41807583](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41807583/); [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-40691-4)
- RWTH Aachen camera-based UAS semi-automatic triage (Drones 2024): 15 subjects, 30 measurements outdoors, 89% triage-class accuracy, F1 = 0.94. The authors call this preliminary basic feasibility only. — [MDPI Drones 8(10):589](https://www.mdpi.com/2504-446x/8/10/589); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/385006049_Automated_Unmanned_Aerial_System_for_Camera-Based_Semi-Automatic_Triage_Categorization_in_Mass_Casualty_Incidents)
- Multi-robot triage platform using onboard sensing and a fine-tuned VLM: ~50 unique victims with human triage labels, two RGB views each (arXiv 2512.08754, late 2025). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.08754)
- ATRACT (arXiv 2605.17123, 2026): drone search and localisation plus wearable vitals, human-in-the-loop, three-level injury output. The authors note that existing systems are "largely fragmented" across localisation, life-sign detection and triage. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.17123)
- Older aerial human-condition and detection datasets **[older]**:
  - NOMAD (natural, occluded, multi-scale aerial persons for emergency response, 2023) — [arXiv 2309.09518](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.09518)
  - "Estimation of Human Condition at Disaster Site Using Aerial Drone Images" (2023) — [arXiv 2308.04535](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.04535)
- Ground-camera rPPG robustness work is relevant to drone footage. For example, it addresses low light and elevated HR — [arXiv 2503.11697](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.11697) — and cross-dataset generalisation (MAE 7.58 on MMPD after training on PURE) — [arXiv 2507.07795](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.07795). I found **no 2025 arXiv paper specifically on rPPG from UAV video**. The aerial-rPPG literature is small.
- NASA technical memo (2024) on uncrewed aerial systems for emergency medical first response (not read in full). — [NASA NTRS](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20240011617/downloads/NASA-TM-20240011617.pdf)

### Inferences
- Delivery is a logistics and regulatory problem more than an AI one. The research value for an AI student lies in **perception, triage and decision support**, not in building another delivery drone.
- Aerial vital-sign estimation has small, mostly indoor, healthy-subject datasets and inconsistent metrics ("accuracy %" vs MAE). That makes it a credible gap for rigorous benchmarking and robustness work: motion, altitude, skin tone, lighting, wind and propeller vibration.
- The DTC finals in Nov 2026 will produce results, and possibly papers and datasets, during the student's 9-month window. Methods should be benchmarked against whatever DARPA releases.

### Gaps
- No independent, peer-reviewed mortality or outcome evaluation of Zipline was found for 2024–2026. The 51% maternal-death figure is unverified.
- Matternet and Wing's 2025–2026 medical programmes (US hospital networks, Ireland, etc.) were not covered by my searches.
- Whether DTC data (de-identified trauma physiology, or Systems-course sensor logs) will be **publicly released** is not stated on DARPA pages.
- The DTC "Accurate Vitals" tolerances (HR ±3, RR ±5) are not confirmed from the primary text.
- Everdrone 2024–2026 Swedish outcome data: none found.

## Q2. Thailand: medical drone projects (MOPH, hospitals, universities, NIEMS/1669, islands/remote areas, floods)

### Takeaway
Thailand moved from scattered demonstrations (Satun, Krabi, Thammasat AED trial 2022) to a **Ministry of Public Health (MOPH)–led pilot programme in 2026**:
- the True "SkyBridge" platform in Nan (Jul 2026) and Mae Chaem, Chiang Mai (Oct 2026);
- island pilots in Phang Nga, Krabi and Satun;
- a Trat pilot with Skyller (Mar 2026);
- a Bangkok national delivery sandbox (late 2025).

All are pilots. Per the Nation (Mar 2026) Thailand has **no commercial medical drone service provider**. No direct NIEMS/1669 drone integration and no Chula, Siriraj or Ramathibodi drone engineering programme was found. Thammasat (Emergency Medicine) and the Thai Resuscitation Council are the academic leads on AED drones.

### Cited Findings
**MOPH + True "SkyBridge" (2026)**
- Launched 16 Jul 2026 at Pua District, Nan. Partners: MOPH, True Corp, CAAT, NBTC, AEROTHAI. The platform does route planning, payload-quality monitoring, real-time tracking, fleet control and automated documentation, and supports multiple drone brands. Routes:
  - NCD-continuity route: 4 km round trip, ~3.5 min by drone vs ~7 min by road.
  - Disaster-response route: 46 km round trip, ~35 min vs >2 h by road.
  - The model will expand to 10 more remote sites.
  - — [True Blog](https://www.true.th/blog/en/true-skybridge-2/); [The Story Thailand](https://www.thestorythailand.com/en/moph-true-skybridge/); [TrueID news (TH)](https://news.trueid.net/detail/n7vwy1mgZZXk)
- Mae Chaem, Chiang Mai (3 Oct 2026), led by Debaratana Vejjanukula (เทพรัตนเวชชานุกูล) Hospital. Missions:
  - (1) NCD home medicine delivery with parachute drop: 14 min by drone vs 1 h 4 min (28 km) by road.
  - (2) Multimodal truck–drone–motorcycle chain to Ban Na Hong health station, used because bridges and roads were cut (69 km combined).
  - (3) Automated long-range eVTOL flight to Mae U-Kho, Mae Hong Son: 100 km round trip in 1 h 9 min vs 188 km and 4 h 40 min by road.
  - The platform reportedly uses **AI analysis of aerial imagery and terrain maps for mission planning** (company/press claim).
  - Next step: Pua Hospital (Nan) with 13 community routes, carrying medicines outbound and **blood and specimens on return flights**.
  - No BVLOS permit details, flight counts, drone specs or budget were disclosed.
  - — [Hfocus Oct 2026 (TH)](https://www.hfocus.org/content/2026/10/39748); [Siam Rath (TH)](https://siamrath.co.th/quality-of-life/326373)

**Island and remote pilots (MOPH / Department of Medical Services)**
- Phang Nga trial at Koh Panyi health-promoting hospital, 29 Mar–14 Apr 2026, also routed Bang Phat pier to Koh Mak Noi. Partners: MOPH, the Air Operations Control Command ISR Centre, and CAAT (working on dedicated medical flight paths).
  - Results: Krabi 20 → ~5 min. Satun cross-sea ~20–30 min. Phang Nga about the same time as by boat, but independent of boat schedules.
  - Payloads cited: medicines, supplies, vaccines, blood.
  - Drone delivery is being considered for inclusion in health-benefit schemes.
  - The article states Thailand has **no commercial medical-drone service provider**.
  - — [Nation Thailand](https://www.nationthailand.com/health-wellness/40064407); [thailand.go.th](https://www.thailand.go.th/public/issue-focus-detail/thailand-tests-medical-drone-delivery-in-phang-nga-to-advance-healthcare-access-in-island-areas)
- Bangkok Post headline: "Thai-built drones tested to speed medicine delivery to remote islands" (article not readable; details **unverified**). — [Bangkok Post](https://www.bangkokpost.com/thailand/general/3210474/thaibuilt-drones-tested-to-speed-medicine-delivery-to-remote-islands)
- Search summaries say MOPH's Department of Medical Services has tested drones since 2023, starting in Satun, and cut transport time from ~120 to 15 min (snippet only, **unverified**). — [Bangkok Post](https://www.bangkokpost.com/thailand/general/3210474/thaibuilt-drones-tested-to-speed-medicine-delivery-to-remote-islands)
- **[older]** Skyports flew a ~12 km BVLOS medical delivery to an island hospital in Satun after CAAT approval; the car-plus-ferry route takes ~55 min. The exact date (≈2022) was not verified. — [Unmanned Airspace](https://www.unmannedairspace.info/latest-news-and-information/skyports-demonstrates-medical-delivery-following-thailand-caas-approval-for-bvlos-flights/); [Bangkok Post](https://www.bangkokpost.com/thailand/general/2584086/drones-fly-medicine-12km-to-island-hospital)

**AED drones in Thailand (academic)**
- Srivilaithon (Thammasat EM), Khunkhlai (Thai Resuscitation Council) and Currie (FlingX). Sci Rep, 26 Feb 2025; flights flown Oct 2022.
  - Setup: DJI Matrice 600, 90 BVLOS flights (487 km) at 80–90 m altitude on the Thammasat Pattaya campus, approved by CAAT.
  - Results: delivery success 88/90 (97.7% in the abstract; one section says 96.7%). Median response 7 min 39 s, median distance 4,042 m, mean speed 9.17 m/s, median landing error 122 cm (max 195 m with a 4 kg payload).
  - Limitations: sunny days only; no real patients; bystander retrieval time not measured; co-author from the operator company.
  - Future work they list: weather, navigation and landing technology, real OHCA trials.
  - — [PMC11865582](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11865582/); [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-91924-x)
- Thai Resuscitation Council "Drone AED Project Phase I" analysed where OHCAs occur in Pattaya to plan drone placement. A Ramathibodi-affiliated author is listed. — [thaicpr.org](https://thaicpr.org/?mod=research&op=view&research_id=8)

**NIEMS / 1669**
- No source found linking drones to the NIEMS (สพฉ.) 1669 dispatch system. Searches returned only general 1669 information. — [NIEMS](https://www.niems.go.th/1/?redirect=True); [Thai PBS](https://www.thaipbs.or.th/news/content/323136)

### Inferences
- Thailand's use cases (NCD drug refills, specimens/blood return legs, islands, mountains, flood-cut roads) are logistics-centred and run by MOPH, True and CAAT. A student is unlikely to get flight access to these, but they could plausibly add **AI layers** such as route and landing-site selection from aerial imagery and terrain, or demand forecasting for NCD refills. They could also **evaluate** these pilots: time savings, cost, specimen integrity.
- AED drones have a Thai evidence base (Thammasat/TRC, Pattaya). The next steps those authors name are real-OHCA trials, landing and navigation, and bystander interaction. **OHCA-location-based drone-base placement modelling for Bangkok or Thai provinces** is a desk-feasible, Thailand-specific gap.
- No Thai aerial-triage or aerial-vital-signs research group was found. Chulalongkorn could plausibly be first to publish in this niche in Thailand, though absence of evidence in my searches is not proof.

### Gaps
- No Chulalongkorn/KCMH, Siriraj or Ramathibodi medical-drone engineering projects surfaced in English or Thai searches. Checking ThaiJO and Chula's engineering faculty and repositories directly would help.
- Drone models, payloads, flight counts, costs and BVLOS permit types for the MOPH 2026 pilots were not disclosed.
- The 2023 Satun DMS baseline (120 → 15 min) is unverified.
- No sources were found on NIEMS plans for drone dispatch.

## Q3. Thai regulatory environment (CAAT, NBTC, BVLOS, other agencies)

### Takeaway
Thailand runs a dual-registration regime: **CAAT** for the aircraft and operator, **NBTC** for radio equipment. It has been tightened twice recently:
- UAS Portal-only registration from 30 Jun 2025;
- new CAAT rules effective **17 May 2026** introducing a risk-assessed "Specific Category", mandatory training, online per-flight permission and 5-year licences.

BVLOS is possible but needs CAAT approval under the **PDRA-101 guidance** (May 2025), which is SORA/JARUS-based. Default BVLOS altitude is ≤90 m AGL in sparsely populated areas. Border-province flight bans have applied since Aug 2025. Indoor micro-drone and simulation work is the realistic path for a student.

### Cited Findings
- Legal base:
  - Ministry of Transport Notification (2015, B.E. 2558) on permission and conditions for remotely piloted aircraft.
  - A 2024 (B.E. 2567) notification for UAVs over 25 kg.
  - Both are cited in CAAT's BVLOS guidance. — [CAAT-GM-UAS-PDRA-101 rev00, 21 May 2025 (TH)](https://uasportal.caat.or.th/docs/guidanceFlight/CAAT-GM-UAS-PDRA101_rev00_21-May-2025.pdf)
- **BVLOS (PDRA-101):** CAAT's guidance for BVLOS via Pre-Defined Risk Assessment.
  - It adopts JARUS SORA and says CAAT may accept PDRAs from EASA.
  - Scenarios: BVLOS ≤90 m AGL over sparsely populated areas; >90 m up to 300 m in sparse areas; and operations within 120 m of trees or structures.
  - Exclusions: communities and dense areas, and outdoor gatherings (stadiums, parks, schools).
  - Weather minima: visibility ≥5 km, no cloud below 300 m, forecast wind ≤25 kt.
  - Applicants must check for aerodromes within 9 km.
  - Required: an operations manual, training, an emergency response plan and an HMI assessment.
  - — [CAAT PDRA-101 PDF](https://uasportal.caat.or.th/docs/guidanceFlight/CAAT-GM-UAS-PDRA101_rev00_21-May-2025.pdf)
- **New rules effective 17 May 2026** (drones ≤25 kg):
  - Mandatory CAAT-approved training (law, ATC rules, safety, risk management).
  - Electronic registration with a visible mark in the format XX-NN-NN-NNNN.
  - Per-operation online flight plan stating purpose, time, coordinates, operators and insurance.
  - A "Specific Category" for medium-risk or non-standard operations, requiring a risk assessment and prior CAAT approval, possibly a manual or demonstration flight.
  - Licence validity extended from 2 to 5 years.
  - Third-party insurance of ≥1M THB per incident.
  - 2015-licensed operators continue until expiry.
  - — [Khaosod English 2026-05-13](https://www.khaosodenglish.com/news/2026/05/13/thailand-tightens-drone-rules-effective-17-may/); [Nation Thailand](https://www.nationthailand.com/news/general/40066155)
- Since 30 Jun 2025 all registrations go through the **CAAT UAS Portal** (uasportal.caat.or.th), and the legacy uav.caat.or.th site was retired. Both CAAT and NBTC registration remain required. — [Pattaya Mail](https://www.pattayamail.com/thailandnews/flying-drones-in-thailand-legal-guide-registration-rules-and-june-2025-caat-update-for-operators-506757); [TAT News Jun 2026](https://www.tatnews.org/2026/06/flying-a-drone-in-thailand-updated-guide-for-tourists/)
- CAAT Announcement No. 4 (28 Aug 2025) banned flights in border and military areas from 31 Aug 2025. TAT (2026) says flights remain prohibited in controlled districts of seven border provinces under CAAT Notice No. 16, unless CAAT approves. One guide's claim of a **nationwide ban in mid-2025** conflicts with TAT and is **unverified**. — [thailand.go.th](https://thailand.go.th/issue-focus-detail/updated-guide-for-tourists-regarding-flying-a-drone-in-thailand); [TAT News](https://www.tatnews.org/2026/06/flying-a-drone-in-thailand-updated-guide-for-tourists/); [drone-laws.com](https://drone-laws.com/drone-laws-in-thailand/)
- Weight thresholds and exam details differ between secondary guides; check the UAS Portal. Commonly cited figures:
  - 9 km airport exclusion;
  - penalties up to 40,000 THB and/or 1 year in prison for flying unregistered;
  - an online exam of about 40 questions, with the pass mark reported as 75% or 80%.
  - — [droneth.or.th](https://droneth.or.th/en/drones-in-thailand/); [dronesgator](https://dronesgator.com/drone-laws-in-thailand)
- NBTC opened public consultation on four draft rules for UAV radio-frequency use (draft, not final). — [The Bangkok Insight](https://www.thebangkokinsight.com/news/digital-economy/1660494/)
- MOPH is coordinating with CAAT on **dedicated medical flight paths**. The SkyBridge pilots list CAAT, NBTC and AEROTHAI (air traffic) as partners. — [Nation Thailand](https://www.nationthailand.com/health-wellness/40064407); [True Blog](https://www.true.th/blog/en/true-skybridge-2/)
- The Thammasat AED trial (2022) obtained CAAT approval for BVLOS flights on a university campus. That shows academic BVLOS research approval is achievable, though the operator was a commercial company (FlingX). — [PMC11865582](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11865582/)

### Inferences
- For a master's student, outdoor BVLOS medical experiments in 9 months are unrealistic without a partner that already holds approval (True, Skyller, FlingX, MOPH). Outdoor VLOS flights with a small registered drone are feasible but need CAAT and NBTC registration, the training/exam, insurance and, under the 2026 rules, per-flight online permission.
- **Indoor flights** (e.g., Crazyflie or Tello in a lab or gym) and **simulation** avoid most airspace-permission issues. I found no explicit CAAT text exempting indoor flight; confirm this with CAAT or the university.
- The "Ministry of Digital Economy" role in the brief was not substantiated. The agencies found were CAAT (aviation), NBTC (spectrum/radio), AEROTHAI (air traffic) and the military (border areas).

### Gaps
- I did not read the full text of the 17 May 2026 regulation (formal title, Open/Specific/Certified thresholds, research or medical exemptions, indoor rules).
- No official statement found on whether indoor flights or flights over private enclosed campus land need registration or permits.
- The official title and number of the Ministry of Transport notification covering BVLOS (cited as clauses 17 and 21) were not retrieved.
- Thai drone-sale or import restrictions (if any) were not found.

## Q4. AI research frontier: autonomous navigation for medical delivery, aerial VLN, casualty detection and triage, simulators, sim-to-real

### Takeaway
UAV **vision-language navigation (VLN)** is growing fast: AerialVLN, CityNav, OpenFly (ICLR 2026), AirNav, UAV-ON and others, plus 2026 surveys. Success rates are still low; AerialVLN models reach about 5% vs about 81% for humans, and OpenFly drops from 33% on seen to 11% on unseen scenes. The surveys name the sim-to-real gap, robust outdoor perception, language ambiguity and efficient onboard models as open problems. Simulator tooling is converging on Unreal-based **Project AirSim/Cosys-AirSim** (classic Microsoft AirSim is deprecated) and NVIDIA **Isaac Sim** (Pegasus Simulator for PX4 multirotors). **Medical-specific** aerial AI (triage, vital signs, delivery-landing safety) has few datasets and no shared benchmark, which leaves room for a student.

### Cited Findings
- Survey "Vision-and-Language Navigation for UAVs: Progress, Challenges, and a Research Roadmap" (arXiv 2604.13654, 15 Apr 2026; Autel, NJU, HKU, PKU, etc.).
  - Four obstacles to deployment: sim-to-real gap; robust perception in dynamic outdoor settings; reasoning with linguistic ambiguity; efficient large-model deployment on constrained hardware. Also continuous 3D actions, long-horizon reasoning and data scarcity.
  - Benchmarks:
    - AerialVLN: 8,446 trajectories and 25,024 instructions; baseline success rate 5.1% vs human 80.8%.
    - CityNav: 32,637 trajectories on real city data.
    - OpenFly: 100k+ trajectories across 18 scenes; success rate 33.2% seen vs 10.7% unseen, and 46.8% with a map vs 22.5% without.
    - AVDN: 3,064 dialog trajectories.
    - UAV-Flow: 30k+ real trajectories.
    - SAGE-Bench: 2M pairs in 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) worlds.
    - Also IndoorUAV, **LogisticsVLN** and BEDI.
  - Simulators listed: AirSim, Gazebo+ROS, CARLA, Unity ML-Agents, AerialVLN Sim, OpenFly (UE/GTA V/Google Earth/3DGS), Isaac Sim, 3DGS environments.
  - Onboard example: GRaD-Nav++ runs onboard at 25 Hz. A 7B OpenVLA is described as computationally expensive. LoRA is used for adaptation.
  - Roadmap: swarms with MLLM planners, air-ground collaboration, world-model/VLA integration, hierarchical "cerebrum/cerebellum" control.
  - — [arXiv 2604.13654](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.13654v1)
- OpenFly integrates 4 rendering engines with an automatic data toolchain and a keyframe-aware VLN model. — [arXiv 2502.18041](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.18041v5)
- AirNav: a large-scale UAV VLN dataset with natural, diverse instructions (arXiv 2601.03707, 2026). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.03707v2)
- UAV-ON: open-world object-goal navigation for aerial agents (arXiv 2508.00288, 2025). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.00288)
- A second 2026 survey covers VLN for aerial robots in the LLM era. — [arXiv 2604.07705](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.07705v1)
- Curated list of resources: [awesome-uav-vln](https://github.com/earth-insights/awesome-uav-vln)
- **Simulators:**
  - Microsoft AirSim (2017) is deprecated with no further updates. Its successor, **Project AirSim**, is now open-source via IAMAI: Unreal Engine 5, Python API, PX4/ArduPilot SITL/HITL. — [Microsoft Research](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/project/aerial-informatics-robotics-platform/); [AirSim docs](https://microsoft.github.io/AirSim/); [ProjectAirSim GitHub](https://github.com/iamaisim/ProjectAirSim)
  - Community forks: Cosys-AirSim (maintained, UE5.5 support) and Colosseum. — [Hivebook](https://hivebook.wiki/wiki/airsim-drone-and-vehicle-simulator-archived-cosys-airsim-fork); [drone-colosseum](https://github.com/gist-ailab/drone-colosseum)
  - **Pegasus Simulator**: an Isaac Sim extension for multiple multirotors with PX4 and ROS2 (ICUAS 2024). Tested with Isaac Sim 4.2.0, PX4 1.14.3 and ArduPilot 4.4; supports multirotors only. — [arXiv 2307.05263](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.05263v2); [GitHub](https://github.com/PhoenixAI-LLC/PegasusSimulator)
- **Low-cost hardware precedents:**
  - DJI Tello search-and-rescue (arXiv 2507.18160, 2025): YOLOv11s person detection at ~15 Hz on an off-board Jetson NX over Wi-Fi, with keypoint-based distance estimation. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.18160)
  - Crazyflie 2.1 with GAP8 (AI-deck): fully onboard CNNs. Object detection reached ~50% mAP at 1.6 fps, and an exploration policy found targets at ~90% in an unseen environment **[older, 2021–2023]**. — [PULP-Frontnet IoTJ](https://iris.polito.it/bitstream/11583/2978558/1/IoTJ_________PULP_Frontnet.pdf); [SUPSI](https://aris.supsi.ch/items/06b23be6-721a-4239-aee0-a4107bc91e51)
- **Medical-specific aerial AI:**
  - Triage and vital signs: see Q1 (Tayfur 2026; RWTH 2024; ATRACT 2026; multi-robot VLM triage 2025).
  - Datasets are small (n ≈ 15–50) and largely not public.
  - The DTC Data competition uses de-identified trauma data, but public release is not stated. — [DARPA](https://www.darpa.mil/news/2025/dart-msai-triumph-darpa-triage-challenge)

### Inferences
- An RTX 4090 Laptop (16 GB) is enough for:
  - running Isaac Sim/Pegasus or Project AirSim at modest scale;
  - training or fine-tuning small detectors and rPPG networks;
  - LoRA fine-tuning of small VLMs (≈2–8B with 4-bit quantisation; this is my estimate, not from a source).
  - It is **not** enough for training large VLA/VLN models from scratch.
- The medical gap on the VLN side is that no benchmark encodes **medical-delivery or casualty-search instructions** ("land at the clinic helipad next to the blue water tank", "find the person lying near the collapsed wall"). LogisticsVLN is the closest general analogue. A small Thai-context medical aerial instruction set in simulation is plausible but would be novel and unvalidated.

### Gaps
- I did not verify the LogisticsVLN details (size, venue) or whether any UAV VLN benchmark already includes medical scenarios.
- No 2025–2026 Crazyflie/Tello work combining onboard person detection with vital-sign estimation was found.
- No synthetic-human aerial rPPG dataset (simulated skin-colour pulse rendered in a drone sim) was found. It may not exist; unconfirmed.

## Q5. Drones in medical and EMS education (training, disaster simulation exercises)

### Takeaway
Evidence is thin and mostly **pre-2021**: small single-site studies using drone footage or drone-assisted SALT triage in MCI simulations, with self-reported gains. I found **no 2024–2025 peer-reviewed study** that directly evaluates drones in EMS or medical education. Recent MCI-education innovation is in digital simulation platforms rather than physical drones, which leaves an education gap that Thailand could fill.

### Cited Findings
- **[older, 2017]** Spain: 35 emergency-nursing master's students reviewed drone aerial footage of a simulated 40-victim MCI. Self-perception changed in 80% (28/35, p = .001). The authors call drones "a great resource" for training. — [PubMed 28658106](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28658106/)
- **[older, 2019 event / 2020 paper]** UCF College of Medicine: drone-assisted SALT triage MCI simulation with 44 participants. Triage accuracy was 52.43% after 15 min of SALT training, with a tendency to under-triage. Median confidence rose from 3/10 to 5/10. Most participants were unaware of drone use. — [PMC7577607](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7577607/)
- 2025 MCI simulation education without drones:
  - Nursing and EMT students' phenomenological study — [PubMed 40254543](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40254543/)
  - Simulation scenario for senior emergency residents, described as "extremely resource and time consuming" — [Clinical Teacher 2025](https://asmepublications.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/tct.70083)
  - Interprofessional MCI simulation — [ScienceDirect 2025](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1555415525002806)
- 2026: the **D2PuLs** digital MCI-training system uses physiological simulation and scene visualisation to bridge "theoretical education, paper-based simulation and resource-intensive full-scale field exercises". — [Frontiers in Disaster & Emergency Medicine 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/disaster-and-emergency-medicine/articles/10.3389/femer.2026.1756349/xml)
- The UK AED-drone simulation (2025) found bystander and call-handler interaction to be the bottleneck. That implies a training need for **dispatchers and laypeople** in drone-AED workflows. — [PMC12355114](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12355114/)
- The Thai SkyBridge pilots explicitly include a "Disaster Response Model" route, but no training or education component was reported. — [True Blog](https://www.true.th/blog/en/true-skybridge-2/)

### Inferences
- A feasible education-oriented capstone: a **simulated drone-view MCI triage trainer**. Students triage casualties from synthetic aerial video, generated in Isaac Sim or Unreal with posed or injured human assets, optionally with AI-suggested START/SALT categories. It could be evaluated with Thai EMS or medical students against a non-drone control. This links physical AI to medical education and needs no flight permits.
- Another option is a dispatcher/bystander training module for drone-AED retrieval and use, based on the Thai Pattaya AED-drone work.

### Gaps
- No Thai study of drones in EMS or medical education was found, including NIEMS training curricula and university disaster drills.
- No 2024–2026 RCT of drone-assisted triage training was found.

## Q6. Stated open problems, and what a single master's student can realistically do in 9 months (RTX 4090 Laptop 16 GB, possibly a small drone, no flight permits)

### Takeaway
The literature consistently names these open problems:
- robust stand-off vital signs (especially respiratory rate) under real conditions such as motion, altitude, lighting, smoke and wind;
- fragmentation between localisation, life-sign detection and triage;
- the sim-to-real gap and poor generalisation to unseen scenes in aerial navigation;
- last-metre problems in delivery: landing precision with payloads, bystander interaction, placement and coverage;
- tiny, non-public medical aerial datasets.

The most feasible high-value projects are **simulation-first plus a small indoor or tethered real-data validation**, avoiding BVLOS entirely.

### Cited Findings (stated open problems)
- Drone vital signs: RR accuracy is markedly lower (82.8–85.2%) than HR, SpO₂ or temperature. Samples are small (n = 37) and mostly indoor. — [PubMed 41807583](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41807583/)
- Aerial triage is preliminary (n = 15, fixed positions). — [MDPI Drones 2024](https://www.mdpi.com/2504-446x/8/10/589)
- Systems are "largely fragmented" across localisation, life-sign detection and triage. — [ATRACT arXiv 2605.17123](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.17123)
- DTC moved to degraded conditions (smoke, darkness, obstruction) in 2025 because real MCIs look like that. — [DARPA news](https://www.darpa.mil/news/2025/dart-msai-triumph-darpa-triage-challenge)
- UAV VLN open problems: the sim-to-real gap, outdoor perception, language ambiguity and onboard efficiency, with large seen/unseen drops (OpenFly 33.2% → 10.7%). — [arXiv 2604.13654](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.13654v1)
- AED drones:
  - landing failures and aborted landings near obstacles; landing error up to 195 m with 4 kg payloads;
  - weather untested; bystander retrieval time unmeasured (Thailand) — [PMC11865582](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11865582/);
  - on-scene delays and bystander support (UK) — [PMC12355114](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12355114/);
  - only 33% of OHCAs eligible for take-off; limited time gains where EMS is fast (Denmark) — [Resuscitation 2025](https://www.resuscitationjournal.com/article/S0300-9572(25)00056-5/fulltext);
  - placement matters ("It's all about location", Cheskes editorial, per the scoping review) — [scoping review](https://sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2666520424002923).
- Thai logistics pilots are pending evaluation of safety, delivery time and service continuity. Benefit-scheme inclusion and dedicated medical flight paths are not yet in place. — [Hfocus](https://www.hfocus.org/content/2026/10/39748); [Nation Thailand](https://www.nationthailand.com/health-wellness/40064407)

### Inferences: feasible project candidates (my synthesis, not sourced claims)
1. **Aerial rPPG/respiration robustness benchmark (simulation plus small real set).**
   - Build a synthetic aerial vital-sign dataset: Isaac Sim/Pegasus or Project AirSim with human avatars whose skin colour and chest motion follow ground-truth HR/RR waveforms.
   - Vary altitude, angle, lighting, smoke and motion blur, plus Thai skin tones.
   - Evaluate classical (POS/CHROM) and deep rPPG models (PhysFormer- or RhythmFormer-style; see [arXiv 2609.03663](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.03663)).
   - Validate on a small IRB-approved real set filmed by a handheld or pole-mounted camera, or an indoor Tello/Crazyflie, at 2–5 m with pulse-ox ground truth.
   - Directly targets the RR weakness and the small-n problem. Compute is fine on a 16 GB GPU.
2. **Aerial casualty detection and START/SALT triage-cue model in degraded conditions.**
   - Fine-tune a YOLO-class detector and pose estimator, or LoRA a small VLM, on public aerial person datasets (e.g., NOMAD) plus synthetic smoke and night renders.
   - Output triage cues: posture, movement, responsiveness proxy.
   - Benchmark against whatever DTC releases after the Nov 2026 finals.
3. **Drone-AED or medical-drone base placement for Thailand.**
   - Use OHCA or 1669 call locations (if obtainable from NIEMS or the TRC Pattaya dataset) plus road-network ambulance times.
   - Optimise drone hub locations (MCLP/p-median or Bayesian coverage, as in [arXiv 2603.23134](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.23134)) for Bangkok, Pattaya or provinces.
   - Pure desk work, policy-relevant, and builds on the Thammasat and TRC studies.
4. **Medical-delivery VLN in simulation.**
   - Small Thai-context instruction set, e.g., "deliver to the rural health promoting hospital (รพ.สต.) beside the temple".
   - Use OpenFly/AerialVLN tooling and test an off-the-shelf VLN or VLM agent for safe landing-zone selection.
   - Higher novelty, higher risk; the 16 GB GPU limits model size.
5. **Education:** a VR or screen-based drone-view MCI triage trainer with AI assist, evaluated with Thai medical or EMS students (Q5). Low hardware risk and needs ethics approval only.
6. **Hardware option:** indoor micro-drones such as a Crazyflie 2.1 with AI-deck, or a Tello EDU (~13 min battery, Python SDK, off-board inference over Wi-Fi as in [arXiv 2507.18160](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.18160)). Suitable for indoor validation without outdoor airspace permission, though indoor exemption should be confirmed with CAAT.

### Gaps
- Availability of Thai OHCA or 1669 geolocated data for research was not checked.
- Public availability of DTC data after the finals is unknown.
- Whether Chulalongkorn already has drone or robotics labs or an IRB precedent for drone-camera physiological recordings was not checked.
- Cost and lead time of Crazyflie AI-deck kits in Thailand were not checked.
