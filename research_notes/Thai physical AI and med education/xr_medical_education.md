# XR (AR/VR/MR) in Medical Education and Clinical Training, and the AI Frontier Inside XR (as of October 2026)

Scope note: research was done on 2026-10-21 through web search and full-text fetches. **Verified** means the number was read from the primary paper or abstract page in this session. **Snippet-level** means it came from a search-result excerpt or a secondary write-up and should be checked before it is cited. **Unverified (background)** means it comes from prior knowledge and was not re-checked in this session. Items from before 2024 are marked [OLDER].

## 1. Evidence base: meta-analyses and RCTs, 2023–2026 (effect sizes, weaknesses)

### Takeaway
Pooled evidence favours XR for **procedural and skills outcomes**, with moderate effects (roughly SMD 0.4–0.7 against conventional teaching). The pooled evidence is **weaker or null for knowledge acquisition with AR/MR** and **null for CPR quality against face-to-face training**. Heterogeneity is very high everywhere (I² often above 85%). Most outcomes are simulator or test scores, not real-patient or operating-room performance, and cybersickness and novelty are recurring confounders.

### Cited Findings

**Broad VR/XR meta-analyses**
- **Verified.** Zhang, Jin, Liu & Tong, *BMC Med Educ* 2025;25:1586 (published 12 Nov 2025), AR/MR meta-analysis of 29 publications, searched to Dec 2023. Against traditional teaching, AR/MR gave:
  - higher skill scores: WMD 12.31 (95% CI 4.12–20.50; I² = 95.8%; 8 studies, n=454)
  - lower failure rate: RD −0.08 (−0.12 to −0.04; I² = 67.4%; 9 studies)
  - shorter performance time: SMD −0.20 (−0.36 to −0.04; I² = 0%; 5 studies, n=582)
  - **no significant knowledge gain:** WMD 2.92 (−1.73 to 7.57; I² = 87.7%; 14 studies, n=826)

  Knowledge became significant only when a live teacher guided the session (WMD 7.15, 0.80–13.50). Heterogeneity fell in the invasive-procedure subgroup (I² = 5.9%). The authors found publication bias for failure rate (P = .017), so that effect may be overestimated. — [Zhang et al. 2025, BMC Med Educ](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12909-025-08166-8)
- **Snippet-level.** A 2025 meta-analysis in *Higher Education Studies* (ccsenet; also indexed in ERIC EJ1484398) reports an overall VR effect of d = 0.510 (95% CI 0.408–0.612) across knowledge, skills and motivation. The venue is lower-tier. — [ccsenet](https://ccsenet.org/journal/index.php/hes/article/view/0/52138); [ERIC](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1484398)
- **[OLDER, 2023] Snippet-level.** A Korean systematic review and meta-analysis gave an overall effect size of 0.72 for skills from VR educational interventions. — [PMC10001289](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10001289/)
- **Snippet-level.** A 2024 meta-analysis and trial sequential analysis compared virtual simulations with mannequins and real persons in medical and nursing RCTs. — [PMC11659697](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11659697/) (effect sizes not extracted: gap)

**Anatomy**
- **Snippet-level.** Results vary by review:
  - An older meta-analysis of 15 RCTs [OLDER, 2020]: SMD 0.53 (95% CI 0.09–0.97; I² = 87.8%). — [PMC7183109](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7183109/)
  - A 2024 VR+AR anatomy meta-analysis reports a smaller pooled effect (SMD about 0.40), largest when VR **supplements** rather than replaces conventional teaching. — [ResearchGate: Efficacy of VR and AR in anatomy education (2024)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/384155881_Efficacy_of_virtual_reality_and_augmented_reality_in_anatomy_education_A_systematic_review_and_meta-analysis)
  - Garcia-Robles et al., *Anat Sci Educ* 2024;17:514–528, is the immersive VR/AR anatomy meta-analysis cited by Zhang et al. — [ref list in Zhang 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12909-025-08166-8)
- **Snippet-level.** A systematic review of 8 RCTs (2025/26) found that VR "consistently matched or exceeded" conventional anatomy teaching. — [PMC13453271](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13453271/)

**Surgery and procedural skills**
- **Snippet-level.** A JMIR 2025 meta-analysis of VR in orthopaedic education found knowledge SMD 1.08 and clinical operation score SMD 1.44. These are training-context outcomes, not OR transfer. — [JMIR 2025 orthopaedic VR MA](https://www.sciencedirect.com/org/science/article/pii/S1438887125014141)
- **Snippet-level.** Evidence for transfer to the operating room is thin. A robotic VR simulator review found only 8 studies eligible for qualitative synthesis and 3 for quantitative synthesis; transfer was not shown for some parameters. — [Surgical Science summary](https://surgicalscience.com/insights/news/significance-of-robotic-vr-simulation-on-operating-room-performance/); [Ortho VR narrative review, PMC12709385](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12709385/)

**Emergency medicine and CPR**
- **Snippet-level.** BMC Med Educ 2024 meta-analysis of RCTs: VR/AR CPR training **did not differ** from face-to-face training on compression depth, rate or overall score (SMD −0.05). The authors call it "as effective", with substantial heterogeneity. — [BMC Med Educ 2024, s12909-024-05720-8](https://bmcmededuc.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s12909-024-05720-8)
- **Snippet-level.** BMC Emerg Med 2025 Bayesian network meta-analysis of XR for CPR: rankings depend on the outcome. VR ranked **lowest** for compression rate (SUCRA 8.9%) and MR ranked highest. — [BMC Emerg Med 2025](https://bmcemergmed.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s12873-025-01256-2)
- **Snippet-level.** The ILCOR Education Implementation Team's systematic review of AR/VR in resuscitation training (Cheng et al., *Resusc Plus* 2024;18:100643) is cited in Zhang 2025. — [ref list](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12909-025-08166-8)

**Communication skills**
- See Section 3 for LLM virtual patients. In the best-reported comparative study, Luo et al. (n=184), an LLM digital patient beat real-patient teaching on history-taking scores (78.13 ± 8.35 vs 67.08 ± 7.21, P<.001). That study is not immersive XR. — [Li & Lutfi 2026 SR, PMC12811743](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12811743/)

**Known weaknesses**
- **Snippet-level (JMIR preprint, not peer reviewed).** A meta-analysis of the psychological effects of VR/AR/MR in medical education found:
  - presence/immersion was highest for VR (SMD 0.78)
  - VR also had the highest cybersickness incidence (28–42%) against AR (≤8%)
  - XR reduced performance anxiety and extraneous cognitive load

  — [JMIR Preprints #100926](https://preprints.jmir.org/preprint/100926)
- **Snippet-level.** A systematic review of 56 I-XR studies: 42.5% of VR studies and 42.9% of AR studies showed greater gains than non-immersive training, and 26.8% showed mixed results. — [PMC12024058](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12024058/)
- **Snippet-level.** A 2025/26 physiology-education XR review says discomfort and motion sickness "may be underreported" and can reduce learning. — [ScienceDirect S2468867326000726](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S2468867326000726)
- **Snippet-level.** A bibliometric study titled "Beyond Novelty to Transfer" (2000–2025, nursing XR) says skill transfer and clinical application are under-studied. — [ScienceDirect S1876139925002075](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1876139925002075)
- **Snippet-level.** A systematic review of cybersickness and presence in healthcare-education VR appeared in *PRESENCE* (MIT Press). — [PRESENCE](https://direct.mit.edu/pvar/article/doi/10.1162/pres_a_00414/120276/The-Effects-of-Virtual-Reality-Used-in-Healthcare)

### Inferences
- XR helps most where the learning target is **spatial-procedural**: invasive procedures, surgical motor skills and the order of steps. It helps least, or not at all, for declarative knowledge, where AR/MR shows no pooled benefit unless an instructor is present.
- Typical effects are moderate, and I² values of 85–96% mean pooled numbers say little about any single implementation. Design quality, dose and comparator choice dominate.
- A Kirkpatrick level 3–4 gap (real-patient behaviour and patient outcomes) persists. Most evidence is satisfaction, test scores or simulator metrics, often from single-site trials with n<100.
- CPR is a cautionary domain: VR alone may train compression rate worse than MR or mannequin feedback, because haptics matter.

### Gaps
- I could not open full texts for the 2024 anatomy meta-analysis (SMD 0.40) or the orthopaedic JMIR meta-analysis to confirm CIs and k. Treat those numbers as snippet-level.
- No 2024–2026 Cochrane-level review of XR surgical training with OR-transfer outcomes was found in this session.
- No pooled estimate of novelty-effect decay (for example, effects at delayed post-test) was found. Few studies report retention beyond 4–8 weeks.

The market is now Meta Quest 3/3S (cheap, open camera API), Apple Vision Pro (M5, premium) and Samsung Galaxy XR (Android XR, Gemini-centric). **Quest 3 with the Passthrough Camera API is the most practical research platform for a laptop-based student.** Among medical XR vendors, Oxford Medical Simulation is the most visibly active (2026 funding aimed at AI scenarios). ## 3. AI + XR frontier

### Takeaway
LLM-driven virtual standardized patients (VSPs) are the most active area. There are dozens of studies, with a 2026 systematic review (39 studies) and early controlled evidence of better history-taking scores. However, few are **embodied in XR**, and fully immersive LLM-VSPs (for example, CLiVR on Quest 3, VAPS at CHI 2025) have only small feasibility samples. AI tutoring in VR surgical simulators has one notable RCT [OLDER, 2022]. Automated assessment from hand and eye kinematics is well established in principle, but mostly older and simulator-bound. VLM-based egocentric procedural guidance (HoloAssist, EgoExo4D) is advancing quickly in CV, but **has almost no medical benchmark**. Gaussian splatting for anatomy is emerging (IEEE TVCG 2025).

### Cited Findings

**LLM and VLM virtual patients**
- **Verified.** Li & Lutfi, *JMIR Med Inform* 2026;14:e79039, systematic review of LLM virtual patients for history-taking: 39 studies (Jan 2020–Aug 2025), English-only.
  - Top-k diagnostic accuracy ranged 0.45–0.98 (34 models, 300 cases).
  - Hallucination rate was 0.31–5%; one fine-tuning study cut it from 3.71% to 0.31%.
  - Information coverage was 33.9–94.4%. SUS ≥80; CUQ 77–86.25; rater κ 0.74–0.83.
  - Controlled evidence:
    - Yamamoto et al. (n=145): OSCE 28.1 vs 27.1, P=.01
    - Luo et al. (n=184): 78.13 vs 67.08, P<.001
    - Brugge et al. (n=21): CRI-HTI 3.60 vs 3.02
  - Limitations: samples usually 10–50 students; only 30% of studies were controlled; about 35% reported fine-tuning details; no meta-analysis was possible; few multimorbidity or rare-condition cases.
  - Proposed thresholds: top-1 ≥0.80, hallucination ≤0.05, SUS ≥80, CUQ ≥75, κ/ICC ≥0.80.
  - Calls for **multilingual, multimodal, emotion-annotated open datasets**.

  — [PMC12811743](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12811743/)
- **Snippet-level.** A JMIR 2025 scoping review of LLM virtual patients (23 studies) found that 21.7% used auxiliary embodiment tools, such as Furhat social robots (Borg et al.). One study found **no performance difference between Chinese and English** VP language combinations. — [JMIR scoping review](https://www.sciencedirect.com/org/science/article/pii/S1438887125014773)
- **Snippet-level.** Yu et al., *Communications Medicine* (Nature, Dec 2025): LLM-agent simulated patient systems were "accurate, readable and reliable". — [Nature Comms Med](https://www.nature.com/articles/s43856-025-01283-x)
- **Snippet-level.** JMIR Med Educ 2026 e92486 is a propensity-matched cohort study of LLM-powered VSPs on undergraduate history-taking. — [JMIR Med Educ 2026](https://mededu.jmir.org/2026/1/e92486)
- **Snippet-level.** "Human or LLM as Standardized Patients? A Comparative Study for Medical Education" (arXiv 2511.14783). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.14783v1)
- **Snippet-level.** CLiVR (arXiv 2510.19031, Oct 2025) runs on **Meta Quest 3** and combines an LLM, ASR/TTS and 3D avatars, with dialogue grounded in a syndrome–symptom database. Latency is about 1.4 s. A user study of 13 medical-school faculty was exploratory. — [arXiv 2510.19031](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.19031v1)
- **Snippet-level.** VAPS (Zhu et al., CHI 2025) is a VR clinical-communication simulator built on LLM-based embodied conversational agents, which faculty can configure. It came out of design requirements from interviews with students. — [arXiv 2503.01767](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.01767v1)
- **Snippet-level.** A GPT-based virtual patient in **mixed reality** for communication training of medical first responders was published as a usability study in JMIR Formative 2024/25. — [JMIR Formative e58623](https://formative.jmir.org/2024/1/e58623/PDF)
- **Snippet-level.** LLMs have been used to grade clinical reasoning in rheumatology VP dialogues (SIGDIAL 2025). — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2025.sigdial-1.56.pdf)
- **Snippet-level.** MDPI *Applied Sciences* 2025 review of LLM VPs in medical and nursing education calls for multi-site controlled studies, standardized metrics and reporting of model versions and prompts. — [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/15/22/11917)
- **Snippet-level.** Curated list: FreedomIntelligence/Awesome-LLM-Patient-Simulators. — [GitHub](https://github.com/FreedomIntelligence/Awesome-LLM-Patient-Simulators)

**AI tutors in VR surgical simulators**
- **Snippet-level.** [OLDER, 2022] Virtual Operative Assistant (VOA), McGill: instructor-blinded RCT with **n=70** medical students. Arms were AI audiovisual metric feedback, remote expert instruction, and no feedback, on a VR neurosurgical tumour-resection simulator.
  - Press release: the AI group learned 2.6 times faster and performed 36% better than the expert-instructed group, with no difference in stress or negative emotion.
  - Against control: 55% less healthy-tissue damage, but about 50% lower hand velocity (less efficient).

  Venue conflict: one search summary said *JAMA Surgery*. The McGill PDF filename ("oi 211346") and my background knowledge point to *JAMA Network Open* 2022 (Fazlollahi et al.). Verify before citing. — [McGill PDF](https://neurosim.mcgill.ca/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/fazlollahi_2022_oi_211346_1644628736.68454-1.pdf); [McGill news](https://www.mcgill.ca/neuro/node/5357); [Stony Brook record](https://researchconnect.stonybrook.edu/en/publications/effect-of-artificial-intelligence-tutoring-vs-expert-instruction-/)

**Automated assessment from hand and eye tracking**
- **Snippet-level.** [OLDER, 2022] MoTOR study, Monash: an MR headset recorded hand and eye movements from 36 participants. Hand path length separated novices, intermediates and experts (left hand 45 vs 31 vs 27 cm, p=.03). — [Monash](https://research.monash.edu/en/publications/assessment-of-core-surgical-skills-using-a-mixed-reality-headset-/)
- **Snippet-level.** 2024 dental VR simulator ("Reflecting on Excellence", **Mahidol University repository**, Thailand): gaze and tool paths were scored automatically. Correct mirror placement and fixation continuity were associated with skill and learning. — [Mahidol repository](https://repository.li.mahidol.ac.th/items/7ed996f1-0306-4552-acc4-877beb617029/full)
- **Snippet-level.** [OLDER, 2022] A deep-learning system classified laparoscopic skill from eye movements. — [PMC9378740](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9378740/)
- **Snippet-level.** [OLDER, 2019] A review of 76 studies on automated technical-skill assessment using tool, hand and eye motion with ML/DL concluded the approach is promising but under-validated. — [Surgical Safety summary](https://www.surgicalsafety.com/clinical-research/automated-methods-technical-skill-assessment-surgery)

**VLM procedural guidance from egocentric AR**
- **Snippet-level.** HoloAssist [dataset OLDER, ICCV 2023]: 166 h of interaction, 350 instructor–performer pairs and 20 manipulation tasks, captured on an AR headset, with mistake and intervention labels. A 2025 technical report (Patsch et al., arXiv 2506.06174) does online mistake detection of procedural and execution errors, plus **LLM-generated explanatory feedback**, and placed 2nd on the HoloAssist challenge. — [arXiv 2506.06174](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.06174)
- **Snippet-level.** Surveys and work from 2025–26:
  - "Building Egocentric Procedural AI Assistant: Methods, Benchmarks, and Challenges" (arXiv 2511.13261, with a GitHub list) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.13261); [GitHub](https://github.com/z1oong/Building-Egocentric-Procedural-AI-Assistant)
  - "Vision-Based Mistake Analysis in Procedural Activities" review (arXiv 2510.19292) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.19292v2)
  - "Proactive Assistant Dialogue Generation from Streaming Egocentric Videos" (arXiv 2506.05904) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.05904)
  - "From Videos to Conversations" (arXiv 2602.01038), which notes the lack of multimodal conversational datasets — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.01038v1)
  - a VLM survey covering egocentric hand-object interaction through embodied AI (arXiv 2608.18671) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.18671)
- **Unverified (background).** Ego4D (2022) and Ego-Exo4D (CVPR 2024) are related egocentric datasets. Ego-Exo4D includes some health-adjacent skilled activities (for example, COVID test and CPR scenarios) and keystep/proficiency annotations. Confirm which medical scenarios it contains before relying on it.

**Gaussian splatting and generative 3D anatomy**
- **Snippet-level.** Kleinbeck et al., "Multi-Layer Gaussian Splatting for Immersive Anatomy Visualization", *IEEE TVCG* 2025 (PubMed 40063492). Layered 3DGS is trained on path-traced CT renderings, and structures can be shown or cut in real time in VR. A follow-up reports a Unity integration with compression for **mobile standalone headsets**. — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40063492/); [emergentmind summary](https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2410.16978)
- **Snippet-level (titles only).** Related 2025–26 work:
  - ClipGS: clippable 3DGS for volumetric medical data (arXiv 2507.06647) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.06647)
  - Hybrid foveated path tracing with peripheral Gaussians for immersive anatomy (arXiv 2601.22026) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.22026)
  - GaussAnything: refining Gaussian scenes on standalone VR (arXiv 2609.13859) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.13859)
  - endoscopic and colonoscopy 3DGS reconstruction (arXiv 2510.18739, 2509.17027) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.18739)

### Inferences
- The **intersection** of "LLM-VSP" and "immersive XR embodiment" is thin. Most LLM-VP evidence is text or 2D, and the XR versions (CLiVR, VAPS, the MR first-responder study) have n≈13–30 and usability-only outcomes. A controlled comparison (XR-embodied vs 2D chat vs human SP) with validated communication rubrics is an open, tractable question.
- AI feedback can **change what learners optimise**: VOA learners were safer but slower. Any AI tutor study should measure trade-offs, not single scores.
- Quest 3 has no eye tracking, so gaze-based assessment needs Vision Pro, Galaxy XR or an add-on. **Hand-joint kinematics from Quest hand tracking** is the accessible substitute.
- Egocentric mistake detection is CV-mature on non-medical tasks (HoloAssist, cooking, assembly). Porting it to **clinical bedside procedures** (hand hygiene, IV cannulation, sterile gloving, CPR) is a clear transfer gap with low hardware needs.

### Gaps
- No 2025–26 study was found combining Quest hand tracking with ML skill classification for a medical procedure. Older work used haptics or HoloLens.
- No medical egocentric mistake-detection benchmark was confirmed in this session.
- No study of **generative (text-to-3D) anatomy** used for teaching was found. Gaussian splatting work is visualisation-focused, without learning-outcome evaluation.
- Emotion and prosody realism in voice LLM-VSPs: no quantitative evaluation was found beyond usability scales.

## 4. Language and culture: Thai and low-resource-language XR and virtual patients; evaluating LLM virtual patients

### Takeaway
**No Thai-language LLM virtual patient or XR virtual-patient system was found** in the peer-reviewed or preprint literature. Thai building blocks exist: Thai medical LLMs (Eir, ThaiLLM), Thai speech-to-speech (Typhoon2-Audio) and Isan ASR (Typhoon Isan). Thai XR education studies are few, small and mostly pre-2024. Evaluation frameworks for LLM VPs are English-centric, and the systematic review explicitly calls for multilingual datasets. This is a clear niche.

### Cited Findings
- **Verified.** The 2026 LLM-VP systematic review **excluded non-English studies** and notes that non-English resources are scarce. — [PMC12811743](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12811743/)
- **Snippet-level.** Evidence that language is not a hurdle for well-resourced languages: no Chinese-vs-English performance difference was found in one LLM-VP study. — [JMIR scoping review](https://www.sciencedirect.com/org/science/article/pii/S1438887125014773)
- **Snippet-level.** Eir is a Thai medical LLM (8B). The authors claim it beats GPT-4o by more than 11% across 18 Thai clinical tasks (self-reported) and that it is deployed on hospital internal networks. — [arXiv 2409.08523](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.08523v2)
- **Snippet-level.** ThaiLLM-8B medical SFT is aimed at citation-grounded QA. — [featherless.ai listing](https://featherless.ai/models/ThaiLLM/ThaiLLM-8B-SFT-IQ)
- **Snippet-level.** A Mahidol preprint on LLM adoption in Thai healthcare lists barriers: compute, privacy, and Thai being a low-resource language. — [medRxiv 2024.06.10.24308731](https://medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.06.10.24308731.full.pdf)
- **Verified (model card).** Typhoon2-Audio (SCB 10X, Dec 2024) is an end-to-end Thai/English speech-in, speech-out model. The LLM and speech-input parts use the Llama 3.1 licence, and the **speech output is CC-BY-NC**. Dependencies are pinned and older. Typhoon Isan offers open ASR for the Isan dialect. — [HF model card](https://huggingface.co/scb10x/llama3.1-typhoon2-audio-8b-instruct); [arXiv 2412.13702 overview](https://www.alphaxiv.org/overview/2412.13702); [SCBX research](https://www.scbx.com/en/category/human-intelligence-en/)
- **Snippet-level.** [OLDER, 2021] Walailak University VR endotracheal intubation study, n=45 in three arms (video; video + VR controllers; video + **VR hand tracking**): both VR arms beat video, and controllers and hand tracking did not differ. — [PMC8616725](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8616725/)
- **Snippet-level.** [OLDER, 2023] Mae Fah Luang University VR basic life support, n=70 (35/35), pre-post design. — [DOAJ BMC Med Educ](https://doaj.org/article/898c8a460aa44f45824ed2d2fdb06b25)
- **Snippet-level (government PR, not peer reviewed).** Chulalongkorn's "ER-VIPE" VR interprofessional ER team training is claimed to cut serious ER errors by up to 38%. — [thailand.go.th](https://thailand.go.th/event-detail/--vr-simulation--38)
- **Snippet-level.** A national survey of Thai simulation-based medical education (2019–20 data) found 6/15 (40%) medical schools had **no simulation centre**. — [SSH news](https://www.ssih.org/news/current-practice-and-needs-assessment-simulation-based-medical-education-medical-education)
- **Snippet-level.** 2024 Mahidol dental VR simulator with gaze and tool-path auto-scoring (see Section 3). — [Mahidol repository](https://repository.li.mahidol.ac.th/items/7ed996f1-0306-4552-acc4-877beb617029/full)

**Evaluating LLM virtual patients: realism, accuracy, safety**
- **Verified.** Metrics used in the field: top-k accuracy, information coverage, hallucination rate, CUQ/SUS, rater κ/ICC and OSCE outcomes. The proposed minimum thresholds are listed in Section 3. — [PMC12811743](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12811743/)
- **Snippet-level.** Reviews call for reporting model versions and prompts, and for data governance. — [MDPI 2025](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/15/22/11917)

### Inferences
- A **Thai-language, voice-based LLM standardized patient on Quest 3** would be novel. It could use Typhoon or another Thai LLM with ASR/TTS, local inference on an RTX 4090 Laptop 16 GB with a quantised 7–8B model, and Thai cultural communication norms (kreng jai, family-centred disclosure, honorifics). An evaluation using the Li & Lutfi metric set, plus Thai-specific cultural-appropriateness ratings, would also fill the explicit "multilingual" gap.
- Thai cultural communication (indirectness, the role of family in breaking bad news, Buddhist framings of illness) is a validity threat for direct translation of Western SP scripts. Culturally grounded case authoring is a contribution in itself.
- The CC-BY-NC licence on Typhoon2-Audio speech output is fine for academic research, but blocks commercial deployment.

### Gaps
- No Thai-specific LLM-VP evaluation (realism, safety, hallucination in Thai) was found. A Thai-language university-database search (ThaiJO) was not done.
- No Thai TTS with emotional prosody was confirmed for 2025–26. A successor to Typhoon2-Audio was not verified.
- Siriraj, Ramathibodi and Chula XR RCTs were not found in this search. They may exist in Thai-language journals.

## 5. Named open problems, and gaps feasible for one master's student (9 months, RTX 4090 Laptop 16 GB, Quest 3)

### Takeaway
Reviews repeatedly name the same open problems: **heterogeneity and non-standard outcomes, small single-site samples, lack of transfer and retention evidence, cybersickness and novelty, English-only LLM-VP evaluation, opaque model and prompt reporting, and no multilingual, emotional or multimodal VP datasets**. Several of these can be tackled by one student with a laptop GPU and a Quest 3.

### Cited Findings (named open problems)
- **Verified.** AR/MR evidence is weakened by high heterogeneity (I² up to 95.8%) and publication bias, and gives no knowledge benefit without an instructor. — [Zhang 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12909-025-08166-8)
- **Verified.** The LLM-VP evidence base has small samples, only 30% controlled studies, inconsistent metrics, poor reporting of training details, few multimorbidity or rare cases, and English-only data. Reviewers call for standardized thresholds, larger cohorts (50–100 students), longitudinal studies and multilingual emotional datasets. — [Li & Lutfi 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12811743/)
- **Snippet-level.** Skill transfer and clinical application are under-studied ("Beyond Novelty to Transfer"). — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1876139925002075)
- **Snippet-level.** Cybersickness may be underreported, at 28–42% in VR against ≤8% in AR (preprint). — [JMIR Preprints #100926](https://preprints.jmir.org/preprint/100926); [physiology XR review](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S2468867326000726)
- **Snippet-level.** Egocentric assistants lack multimodal conversational datasets. — [arXiv 2602.01038](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.01038v1)
- **Snippet-level.** Real-time high-quality anatomy rendering is impractical on mobile headsets, which motivates 3DGS. — [Kleinbeck 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40063492/)
- **Snippet-level.** AI tutors can trade efficiency for safety, and the optimal hybrid AI-plus-human instruction is still being studied. — [McGill VOA](https://www.mcgill.ca/neuro/node/5357)

### Inferences: candidate thesis projects (feasibility judgments are mine, not sourced)

1. **Thai voice LLM standardized patient in Quest 3 MR (high feasibility, high novelty).**
   - Build: Unity on Quest 3, ASR, a Thai LLM (local quantised 7–8B on the 4090, or an API), TTS and an avatar.
   - Evaluate with the Li & Lutfi metrics (information coverage, hallucination, CUQ/SUS, rater κ) plus Thai cultural-appropriateness ratings.
   - Run a small randomised crossover against text-only chat (n≈30–60 students).
   - Fills: the multilingual gap, the immersive-embodiment-vs-2D gap, and the standardized-metrics call.
2. **Benchmark and safety audit of LLM VPs in Thai (very high feasibility, laptop-only).**
   - Build 30–50 Thai cases and run several LLMs (Typhoon, Eir, open multilingual models, frontier APIs) as patients.
   - Measure case fidelity, hallucinated findings, persona drift across turns, and emotional consistency, judged by clinicians plus an LLM-as-judge with agreement statistics.
   - Release the dataset. This is a publishable dataset paper, and it can be Phase 1 of project 1.
3. **Hand-tracking-based automatic skill assessment on Quest 3 (moderate feasibility).**
   - Log 26-joint hand kinematics during a simple VR or MR procedure (for example, sterile gloving, suturing on a pad, or the CPR hand position and rate with a mannequin in passthrough).
   - Train lightweight sequence models (GRU or transformer) to classify novice vs expert, or predict OSATS-style scores. Report the validity argument (Messick/Kane).
   - Caveat: recruiting experts takes time, and hand tracking degrades with occlusion and gloves.
4. **Egocentric VLM step and mistake detection for a bedside procedure using Quest passthrough frames (moderate feasibility, higher risk).**
   - Record a small dataset (for example, WHO hand hygiene steps, or IV cannulation on a trainer arm) through the Passthrough Camera API (30 fps, 1280×960).
   - Fine-tune or prompt a small VLM on the 4090 for step recognition and error flags, and overlay feedback in MR.
   - Fills the gap that HoloAssist-style mistake detection has no medical benchmark. Latency (40–60 ms camera plus inference) and the 16 GB VRAM limit mean small models or keyframe sampling.
5. **Gaussian splatting anatomy on standalone Quest 3 with a learning-outcome evaluation (moderate feasibility).**
   - Reproduce multi-layer 3DGS from open CT data, deploy with compression, and run a small RCT against a mesh-based app or atlas.
   - Fills the gap that 3DGS anatomy papers are visualisation-only.
6. **Cybersickness and novelty-controlled study design (add-on).** Any of the above can add SSQ/VRSQ measures, a delayed post-test (4–6 weeks) and a familiarisation session to address the novelty critique cheaply.

### Gaps
- No primary source in this session gave numbers on IRB timelines or student-recruitment feasibility in Thai medical schools.
- Medical-domain egocentric datasets (for example, any surgical or clinical subset of Ego-Exo4D) were not verified.
- Whether Thai-capable open VLMs and LLMs fit 16 GB VRAM with acceptable latency on-device was not benchmarked here. This needs a quick empirical check (for example, 4-bit 7–8B models, which typically fit in 16 GB).
