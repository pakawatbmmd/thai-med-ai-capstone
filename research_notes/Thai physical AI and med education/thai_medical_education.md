# Thai Medical Education Landscape and Needs (as of Oct 2026), context for an AI / physical-AI / XR capstone at Chulalongkorn

Research date: 21 Oct 2026. Thai Buddhist Era (BE) years converted: 2568 BE = 2025 CE, 2569 BE = 2026 CE. Items older than 2023 are marked **[older]**. "Verified" = read on the primary/near-primary page; "snippet-only" = seen only in a search-result summary, not opened.

## 1. Structure: medical schools, rural tracks, licensing exam (CMA), OSCE, internship and service obligation, nursing and EMS training

### Takeaway
Thailand has 29 Medical Council-accredited MD programs and produced about 3,170 new doctors in 2025. Most graduates owe 3 years of compulsory public service, and about a third of bonded graduates (1,012) come through the rural-recruitment CPIRD track. Licensure is a 3-step national exam run by the Center for Medical Competency Assessment and Accreditation (CMA). Step 3 is a Thai-language OSCE, which makes clinical-skills assessment a high-stakes, resource-heavy bottleneck. The EMS/paramedic pipeline is much thinner than the physician pipeline: there are 1,790 paramedics nationally and a target of 7,500.

### Cited Findings
**Medical schools and output (verified, 2026)**
- 29 Thai medical schools have Medical Council-accredited MD programs, and the Council recognizes 104 specialty training programs. Source: Medical Council seminar at MOPH on 2 July 2026, reported by Hfocus — [Hfocus, 2 Jul 2026](https://www.hfocus.org/content/2026/07/38586)
- New graduates in 2025: 3,170 total (2,987 public schools, 183 private, 186 foreign-trained). 2,843 carry compulsory-service obligations, of whom 1,720 came through the consortium (กสพท)/international track and 1,012 through CPIRD. Actual placements were 2,486 in MOPH units and 686 in other government agencies, and 271 had incomplete qualifications — [Hfocus, 2 Jul 2026](https://www.hfocus.org/content/2026/07/38586)
- National production target is about 3,000 per year, with about 1,980 going to MOPH in 2025. The Cabinet-approved manpower strategy targets a doctor-to-population ratio of 1:650 by 2032, and the Medical Council suggests 1:600 — [Hfocus, 2 Jul 2026](https://www.hfocus.org/content/2026/07/38586)
- New schools are still being added. The Cabinet reportedly approved a Faculty of Medicine at Nakhon Phanom University in April 2026, budgeted at 2.502 bn THB over FY2027–2036, with a first cohort in 2028 (snippet-only; not opened) — [Thairath English](https://en.thairath.co.th/news/politic/2929373). Praboromarajchanok Institute (MOPH) also plans its own faculty of medicine (snippet-only) — [The Thaiger](https://devth.thethaiger.com/th/news/national/praboromarajchanok-institute-plans-faculty-of-medicine-to-ease-healthcare-professional-shortage)
- Bonded MOPH placements by fiscal year, shown as graduates / MOPH allocated / actually worked: FY2018 2,648/1,994/2,016; FY2019 2,629/2,054/2,044; FY2020 2,636/2,031/2,039; FY2021 2,610/2,023/2,021; FY2022 2,618/1,849/1,850 — [ThaiPBS, 8 Jun 2023](https://www.thaipbs.or.th/now/content/563206)

**Rural tracks: CPIRD and ODOD**
- CPIRD (Collaborative Project to Increase Production of Rural Doctors) recruits students with rural backgrounds and trains them clinically in MOPH regional and provincial hospitals. ODOD (One District One Doctor) is the companion scheme — [Education for Health](https://educationforhealthjournal.org/index.php/efh/article/view/8/88)
- **[older, 2016]** Over 20 years, CPIRD and ODOD together produced 5,926 doctors — [PMC5198815](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5198815/)
- CPIRD graduates have the highest retention in the public system, cited at 80–90%. MOPH's first 2026 proposal is to fold all new production into the CPIRD system — [Hfocus, 2 Jul 2026](https://www.hfocus.org/content/2026/07/38586)

**National licensing exam (CMA / CMCAA)**
- The exam is a 3-step model adapted from the US system: pre-clinical knowledge, clinical knowledge, and clinical skills — [Global Health Action via DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/ffc80414b85b487c9249d9e31f24fd89) **[older, 2014]**
- Timing: Step 1 at the end of Year 3, Step 2 at the end of Year 5, Step 3 at the end of Year 6. Step 1 covers the integrated pre-clinical curriculum and is considered the hardest (Thammasat paper; snippet-only) — [thaiscience.info JMAT](https://thaiscience.info/journals/Article/JMAT/10987766.pdf)
- Parts 1 and 2 are MCQ in English. Part 3 is an OSCE conducted almost entirely in Thai, which is the main hurdle for overseas-trained candidates. This comes from a secondary 2026 guide and is unverified against CMA — [uni2study](https://www.uni2study.com/articles/thailand-medical-council-licensing-exam-abroad-graduates)
- Step 1 comprises about 300 MCQs aligned to the Medical Competency Assessment Criteria for National License (2012). The CMA mean passing score for main rounds from 2019 to 2024 was 52.3%, ranging from 51.0% in 2024 to 53.0% in 2021 — [Saowaprut et al., JEEHP 2025](https://www.jeehp.org/journal/view.php?number=562)
- **[older, 2015]** An observed long case was added to the national exam in 2007. The authors note it is less reliable than an OSCE — [Vajira Med J](https://he02.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/VMED/article/view/54156)

**Internship and compulsory service**
- Graduates of public schools owe 3 years of public service (ใช้ทุน), usually starting with an internship year in MOPH hospitals. Placements are tabulated above.
- **[older, 2013–2022 data]** Of 19,355 permanently appointed MOPH doctors, resignations were 226 in internship year 1 (1.2%), 1,875 in year 2 (9.69%), 858 in year 3 (4.4%), and 1,578 after completing service (8.1%) — [ThaiPBS, 2023](https://www.thaipbs.or.th/now/content/563206)

**Nursing**
- About 10,000 nurses graduate each year, but about 7,000 leave state facilities for better pay. Workloads reach 80 h/week, and there were 184,400 registered nurses in 2021. This is a news source, not primary data — [AsiaNews](https://www.asianews.it/news-en/Thailand's-state-run-hospitals-hard-hit-by-nurse-shortages-58558.html)
- **[older, 2018]** A health-demand projection to 2026 found supply adequate for most professions "except nurses who will be in critical shortages" — [Human Resources for Health 2018](https://human-resources-health.biomedcentral.com/counter/pdf/10.1186/s12960-018-0336-2.pdf)

**Paramedic / EMS (verified, Sept 2026)**
- EMS personnel: 7,449 professionals, including 1,798 emergency physicians, 3,779 emergency nurse practitioners and 1,790 paramedics. Support staff exceed 135,353, including 111,605 emergency medical volunteers. A Senate working group wants to raise paramedics to 7,500 within 5 years through a "Paramedic Sandbox" — [Khaosod English, 13 Sep 2026](https://www.khaosodenglish.com/news/2026/09/13/thailand-emergency-medical-system-faces-major-gaps-as-2574-subdistricts-lack-units/)
- In FY2025, 2,574 subdistricts (34.6%) lacked adequate standard response units. Coverage in the Central region is 46.6%. There are 1.76 advanced units per 100,000 people. The target is an 8-minute response for critical patients. Digital systems include the 1669 hotline, NDEMS and RNIS dispatch. There are 37 air medical units, and 956 people were trained in 21 Basic HEMS courses between FY2022 and FY2026 — [Khaosod English, 13 Sep 2026](https://www.khaosodenglish.com/news/2026/09/13/thailand-emergency-medical-system-faces-major-gaps-as-2574-subdistricts-lack-units/)
- An undated Mahidol page cites a NIEM estimate that Thailand needs more than 35,000 paramedics within 5 years. Treat it with caution — [Mahidol GIVE](https://give.mahidol.ac.th/?p=1066)
- Basic EMT courses are run with the National Institute for Emergency Medicine (NIEM/สพฉ.), including at private hospitals — [Pattaya Mail](https://www.pattayamail.com/ourcommunity/bhp-turns-out-33-more-fully-qualified-paramedics-15469)

### Inferences
- The Step 3 OSCE and the school-level OSCEs before it need many standardized patients and examiners, and have to run in Thai. That makes AI-assisted OSCE practice and scoring, such as a Thai-language virtual standardized patient or automated checklist scoring from video, a plausible high-value niche.
- CPIRD/ODOD trainees spend clinical years in regional MOPH hospitals with less simulation infrastructure than Bangkok centres. Portable or remote tools (XR, low-cost sensorized task trainers, tele-debriefing) map well onto this distributed model.
- EMS is the most acute pipeline gap: a 4x paramedic scale-up target and a reliance on volunteers. Scalable training such as VR mass-casualty triage, AI-graded CPR/airway feedback, or drone-AED dispatch simulation has a clear policy hook.

### Gaps
- I did not reach the official CMA site (cmathai.org or similar). Current pass rates, Step 3 OSCE station counts and format, and any 2025–2026 exam reforms are unverified.
- No official count of ODOD vs CPIRD graduates for 2025 was found. The Hfocus report covers CPIRD only.
- I found no current (2025–2026) Nursing Council figure for the nurse shortage. Paramedic degree-program numbers (how many universities and annual graduates) were also not found.
- The Nakhon Phanom faculty approval was seen only as a search snippet.

## 2. Workforce problems: shortage, maldistribution, retention, burnout, intern attrition, instructor ratios, ageing society

### Takeaway
MOPH says Thailand is still short about 14,000 doctors (July 2026). About 1,000 doctors leave the public system each year, and nearly half of all doctors are concentrated in Bangkok. Interns have very high burnout: 48% overall, with 81% working more than 80 hours per week and 79% considering resignation. Simulation programs report faculty and technician shortages, which is the clearest opening for technology that saves faculty time.

### Cited Findings
- 84,487 doctors are registered and 80,256 are living and licensed. 37,645 are in Bangkok and 41,612 in the provinces. MOPH employs about 25,000 doctors, of whom about 21,000 are in active practice. The need is 35,000 FTE by workload or 28,000 by the manpower strategy, and the shortage is put at 14,000 (MOPH). About 1,000 doctors leave the system each year. GP numbers have stayed flat at about 8,000 for 5 years, while specialists grow by 500–1,000 a year — [Hfocus, 2 Jul 2026](https://www.hfocus.org/content/2026/07/38586)
- About 13,000 doctors are over 60 — [Hfocus, 2 Jul 2026](https://www.hfocus.org/content/2026/07/38586)
- National ratio is about 1 doctor per 2,000 people. Bangkok has about 1:462, while some provinces are at about 1:5,000. More than 450 doctors are lost each year (government portal; snippet-only, undated) — [thailand.go.th](https://www.thailand.go.th/issue-focus-detail/thailands-healthcare-system-faces-critical-turning-point-driven-by-emerging-medical-innovations)
- **[2023]** Average MOPH resignations were about 455 per year plus 150–200 retirements (2013–2022). The article gives both 655 and 665 as the total, so it is internally inconsistent. In 2022, 4,040 of 24,668 MOPH doctor posts were on study leave. A survey found 65 hospitals where doctors worked more than 40 h/week beyond regular hours — [ThaiPBS, 2023](https://www.thaipbs.or.th/now/content/563206)
- One counter-view holds there is no absolute shortage, only maldistribution and poor management. An NHCO official argued production of about 3,000 a year is sufficient. This is **[older, likely ~2017]** — [Bangkok Post](https://bangkokpost.com/thailand/general/1227064/no-doctor-shortage-says-health-panel). It is contradicted by the 2026 MOPH figure of a 14,000 shortage in [Hfocus](https://www.hfocus.org/content/2026/07/38586)
- Intern burnout, from a national survey (June–July 2022; n=412, response rate 15.3%; published 2025):
  - Overall burnout 48.1%. High emotional exhaustion 83.5%, high depersonalization 74.8%, low personal accomplishment 66.5%.
  - Mean 69.3 h/week, and 81.2% worked more than 80 h/week.
  - 78.6% considered resigning and 11.9% actually resigned within the year.
  - Burnout was associated with self-rated poor performance (aOR 2.14). High depersonalization was associated with clinically harmful errors (aOR 2.21).
  - Lack of colleague support (aOR 2.56) and no academic counselling (aOR 2.11 for low accomplishment) were risk factors.
  — [BMC Public Health 2025, PMC12117941](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12117941/)
- A separate MDPI study on burnout and quality of work life among Thai interns exists; details not extracted — [Behav Sci 2024](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-328X/14/5/361)
- **[older, 2018]** Doctors leaving before finishing 3-year service number about 500–600 per year, described as a decades-long chronic problem (Thai news, via search summary) — [Matichon](https://www.matichon.co.th/local/education/news_1029629)
- Ageing society: Thailand will need up to 250,000 additional paid care workers by 2037, and paid home-based care demand will rise by at least 70% in 12 years — [ILO, 2025](https://www.ilo.org/resource/news/thailand-faces-shortfall-250000-care-workers-2037-ilo-report-finds)
- Instructor and simulation staffing, from a national SBME survey (data 2019–2020):
  - Simulation technician shortage was reported by 93.3% of administrators, and researcher shortage also by 93.3%.
  - Insufficient faculty was reported by 74.4% of instructors and insufficient faculty training by 59.1%.
  - Students wanted about 6 learners per session, versus the 15.4 instructors reported.
  — [Boonmak et al., BMC Med Educ 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9019967/) **[older data]**

### Inferences
- The binding constraint for skills training is faculty and technician time, more than equipment. AI that gives automated, objective feedback on procedural skills (CPR, IV, suturing, ultrasound probe handling) or automates debriefing can multiply scarce instructors. That is a measurable outcome: instructor-minutes per competent learner.
- Interns' burnout predictors (lack of support and counselling) and the link to harmful errors suggest a niche for just-in-time procedural refreshers or AI coaching for interns in rural hospitals. Any such tool must be tested carefully for workload burden.
- Ageing-society demand points to training non-physician carers and nurses (geriatric care, rehab, home care) as a capstone direction. It also links to KCMH's rehab-robot work (see Q5).

### Gaps
- No 2025–2026 national data on doctor attrition specifically during internship year 1 was found. ThaiPBS covers 2013–2022.
- No national faculty-to-student ratio for Thai medical schools was found.
- The thailand.go.th ratio figures were snippet-only, because the page did not render.

## 3. Simulation infrastructure (Chula, Siriraj, Ramathibodi, CMU, KKU, PSU); use of VR/AR, mannequins and SPs

### Takeaway
Chula's simulation hub is CHAMPS, the Chulalongkorn Healthcare Advanced Multi-Profession Simulation Center. It merged the Clinical Skills and Simulation Center (CSSC) and the Simulation & CPR Training Center (SCTC), and reportedly holds SSH accreditation. Siriraj runs SiTEC and SiMSET. Nationally, part-task trainers and standardized patients dominate, high-fidelity mannequins are moderate, and screen-based or virtual simulation is rare (about 11–16%). In 2019–2020, 40% of responding schools had no simulation center.

### Cited Findings
**Chulalongkorn / KCMH**
- CHAMPS was formed by merging two centres:
  - CSSC, on floor 11 of the Bhumisiri Mangkhalanusorn Building since 2017. It trains medical students in clinical, communication, physical-exam and procedural skills.
  - SCTC, on floor 16 of the Sor Thor Building at KCMH, opened 2018. It trains residents, nurses, EMTs and hospital staff, and runs public CPR courses.
  - CHAMPS trains standardized patients and runs the "SmartSim" online system for booking, mannequin loans, self-practice and SP search. No AI or XR initiatives are listed.
  — [CHAMPS site](https://champs.md.chula.ac.th/)
- CSSC was founded under Dean Prof. Adisorn Patradul (2007–2011). It is described as Thailand's first medical training center to receive world-standard accreditation, and KCMH says the center holds SSH Teaching/Education accreditation (snippet-only; year unverified) — [Chula highlight](https://www.chula.ac.th/en/highlight/62197); [KCMH Simulation & CPR Center](https://kcmh.chulalongkornhospital.go.th/en/ec/simulation-cpr-center-sctc-en/)

**Siriraj (Mahidol)**
- SiTEC (Siriraj Training and Education Center for Clinical Skills) was established in 2011 and focuses on surgical skills with cadavers and simulation. SiMSET is the broader simulation centre, established in 2015. Siriraj's Medical Education Technology Center reports immersive-tech work (AR/VR/MR), including a 2023 poster on 3D AR for residents — [SiMSET history](https://www2.si.mahidol.ac.th/en/?p=5082); [Siriraj Med Ed Technology Center](https://www2.si.mahidol.ac.th/en/medical-education-technology-center/)

**Ramathibodi / Mahidol**
- **[older, 2022]** Jolly Good (Japan) and Mahidol ran a VR infectious-disease training demonstration across 4 units: the Faculty of Tropical Medicine, Ramathibodi School of Nursing, Ramathibodi Faculty of Medicine, and the Educational Simulation Center at Chakri Naruebodindra Medical Institute — [Healthcare IT News](https://www.healthcareitnews.com/news/asia/roundup-thailands-mahidol-university-begin-vr-training-infectious-disease-treatment-and); [BioSpectrum Asia](https://www.biospectrumasia.com/news/56/21303/thailand-based-mahidol-university-adopts-virtual-reality-for-medical-education.html)
- Ramathibodi nursing presented on 360° VR simulation for patient safety in nursing education at a 2023 conference (Thai page) — [Rama Nursing](https://www.rama.mahidol.ac.th/nursing/th/node/4156)

**National picture (Boonmak et al., BMC Med Educ 2022; data Aug 2019–Jul 2020) [older]**
- 22 schools were contacted, and administrators from 15 responded. Instructors from 16 schools responded (186, of whom 82.8% use SBME), and 367 sixth-year students were analysed.
- 40% of responding schools had no simulation center, and 60% had no simulation administration system.
- Simulator use reported by students: part-task trainers 96.7%, standardized patients 90.2%, cadaver 69.5%, high-fidelity mannequin 54.5%, screen-based simulation 16.3%. Clinical instructors reported screen-based simulation at 11.3%. VR is not mentioned at all.
- Barriers reported by administrators: screen-based simulator shortage 80%, high-fidelity mannequin shortage 66.7%, technician shortage 93.3%.
- Students rated simulation's benefit to clinical practice at 4.23/5, and 99.5% had practiced BLS and ALS via simulation.
- Authors span KKU, Siriraj, PSU, Chulabhorn Royal Academy, Ramathibodi, Thammasat, Phramongkutklao and CMU.
— [PMC9019967](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9019967/)

### Inferences
- Thai schools rely heavily on human standardized patients and part-task trainers, and virtual or screen-based simulation is scarce. A capstone that adds AI or XR on top of existing part-task trainers would fit existing CHAMPS workflows and its SmartSim self-practice and loan system. Examples include sensorized mannequin feedback, AR overlays on task trainers, and a computer-vision skills scorer. This would be easier to adopt than replacing infrastructure.
- CHAMPS already serves medical students, residents, nurses, EMTs and the public, which makes an interprofessional or EMS-oriented project feasible inside one centre.

### Gaps
- No primary 2023–2026 data was found on simulation centres at Chiang Mai, Khon Kaen or Prince of Songkla. Their participation in the national SBME survey is the only evidence.
- Equipment inventories (e.g., counts of high-fidelity mannequins or VR headsets) and annual throughput for CHAMPS, SiMSET and others were not found.
- The SSH accreditation year for CHAMPS/CSSC was not verified.
- No Thai SBME survey newer than 2019–2020 data was found.

## 4. Existing AI / XR / robotics in Thai medical education, and LLMs on Thai exams

### Takeaway
Thai evidence is thin and recent. The strongest study is a 2025 Ramathibodi paper showing frontier LLMs pass a ThaiNLE Step 1 mock: GPT-4o 88.9%, against a human pass mark of about 52%. Thai XR education studies are small (e.g., a 2025 Thammasat AR RCT with n=24). No Thai RCT of VR or AI-chatbot tutoring for medical students was found. Institutionally, AI in medical education became an explicit Chula priority in 2026 through the BRIDGE-AI Summit.

### Cited Findings
- **LLMs on ThaiNLE Step 1** (Saowaprut, Wabina, Yang, Siriwat; Ramathibodi Clinical Epidemiology; JEEHP 2025):
  - Dataset: a 304-question English mock exam, 10.2% with images.
  - Micro-accuracy: GPT-4o 88.9%, GPT-4 83.3%, Claude-3.5-Sonnet 80.1%, Claude-3-Opus 77.8%, Gemini-1.5-Pro 72.8%, Gemini-1.0-Pro 61.4%. All exceed the 2019–2024 CMA mean pass mark of 52.3%.
  - Image questions were mostly lower (GPT-4o 82.6% vs 89.6% on text-only).
  - Limitations: restricted dataset, only 31 image questions, MCQ only, and no human comparison.
  — [JEEHP 2025;22:16](https://www.jeehp.org/journal/view.php?number=562); preprint [medRxiv Dec 2024](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.12.20.24319441v1.full-text)
- **[2023]** Tangadulrat, Sono and Tangtrakulwanich (Prince of Songkla University) ran a cross-sectional survey of Thai medical students' and physicians' views on ChatGPT in clinical practice and education. The results could not be retrieved because the page did not render — [JMIR Med Educ 2023;9:e50658](https://mededu.jmir.org/2023/1/e50658/authors)
- **2025 AR RCT (Thammasat):** a stratified RCT of AR handouts vs traditional handouts for microbiology in 3rd-year medical students. It measured MCQ scores, MSLQ motivation and satisfaction, with n=24 (12 per group) — [PubMed 40996281](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40996281/)
- A Thai pathology study compared ChatGPT and Bard on image-based pathology exam questions (Apornvirat, Namboonlue, Laohawetwanit; Am J Clin Pathol 2024), cited in the JEEHP references — [JEEHP reference list](https://www.jeehp.org/journal/view.php?number=562)
- A KMITL study built a Thai-language VR first-aid/CPR+AED game (snippet-only; year not confirmed) — [KMITL DSpace](https://dspace.kmitl.ac.th/handle/123456789/14887)
- Siriraj has a 2023 AR poster for residents (see Q3), and Mahidol/Jolly Good ran a VR demonstration in 2022 (see Q3).
- **BRIDGE-AI Summit 2026** (20–21 July 2026, KCMH), organised by the Division of Integrative Innovation and Digital Technology, Faculty of Medicine, Chula, with the Cornell–Chula "C2Gether" Institute (Weill Cornell):
  - Day 2 was devoted to AI in medical teaching, learning and assessment. It included a debate on routine AI use by learners and faculty, and a talk on institutional AI guidelines (IMU Malaysia).
  - The summit aimed to produce a "Bangkok BRIDGE-AI Declaration 2026" as an ASEAN reference framework.
  - The competition had a "Medical Education Award" category covering simulation and curriculum innovation. Track A (high school/undergraduate) had 30 teams and Track B (graduate/residents) 60 teams, with 180 applicants.
  - Chula Engineering panelists: Asst. Prof. Natawut Nupairoj (Computer Engineering), Assoc. Prof. Ronnapee Chaichaowarat (robotics) and Assoc. Prof. Atiwong Suchato (AI/NLP).
  — [BRIDGE-AI Summit 2026 site](https://mdcuconference.com/event/sm/content/14/TH/home)

### Inferences
- LLMs already exceed the ThaiNLE Step 1 pass mark on English MCQs. MCQ-tutor chatbots are therefore no longer novel and pose an assessment-integrity issue. The open problems are in multimodal and Thai-language clinical reasoning, plus OSCE-style communication in Thai, where no Thai evidence exists.
- Thai published XR studies are small single-site pilots. A capstone with a properly powered, pre-registered evaluation (even n of about 60–100) could be among the more rigorous Thai studies in this space.
- BRIDGE-AI shows Chula Faculty of Medicine leadership is actively seeking AI-in-medical-education innovations. Its competition categories and named faculty are natural advisors and venues.

### Gaps
- No Thai 2024–2026 survey of medical students' generative-AI use, prevalence or policy was found. Only the 2023 PSU survey exists, and its results were not retrieved.
- Thai journals (Thai J Med Educ, Siriraj Med J, Chula Med J, J Med Assoc Thai) were not systematically searched because of tool-call limits. Thai-language VR/AR/AI education papers there are likely and unindexed here.
- No study was found of LLMs on ThaiNLE Step 2/3 or on Thai-language versions.
- No published Thai study was found on robotic or physical-AI training devices for education.

## 5. Chula's "AI & Digital Healthcare" master's, related labs, and likely partners

### Takeaway
The program's official name is the MSc in Digital and AI Technologies in Health Systems (หลักสูตรวิทยาศาสตรมหาบัณฑิต สาขาวิชาเทคโนโลยีดิจิทัลและปัญญาประดิษฐ์ในระบบสุขภาพ), an interdisciplinary program. It is a 1-year, part-time joint program of the Faculty of Medicine and (per its description) computer engineering, with industry and MOPH partners. Named leadership was not found. The nearest Chula physical-AI and robotics resources are Assoc. Prof. Ronnapee Chaichaowarat's Intelligent Robotics Lab at ISE and KCMH's Physical-AI robot program with True Digital (Jan 2026). The new Chula AI Center (July 2026) and the Chula–NVIDIA AI Technology Center partnership supply AI infrastructure.

### Cited Findings
- **Program details:** 1 academic year, out-of-hours study. It is a joint program of 2 faculties that integrates medicine with computer engineering, with focus areas of AI, Big Data, IoT, digital health, ethics and precision medicine. It works with industry experts and MOPH. Partner logos include True, Gulf, MTL and Princ Hospital Suvarnabhumi (inferred from image filenames). Admission is for AY2569 (2026), contact digihealth@chula.md. Director, curriculum, tuition and capstone requirements are not listed — [digihealth.md.chula.ac.th](https://digihealth.md.chula.ac.th/)
- **Separate program:** an MD + MEng (Biomedical Engineering) dual degree in 6 years was announced in 2020 **[older]**. It is not the same as the digital-health MSc — [Chula news](https://www.chula.ac.th/news/148638/)
- **KCMH Physical AI robots (launched 30 Jan 2026):** Faculty of Medicine and KCMH with True Digital.
  - Robots: an upgraded robot for radioactive-iodine thyroid cancer treatment, and a "Medical Robot for Treatment and Physical Rehabilitation" for TB patients.

## 6. Funding, policy and ethics

### Takeaway
Policy momentum is strong.
- Thailand's National AI Strategy and Action Plan (2022–2027) names healthcare as a priority sector.
- A 25 bn THB AI package (2025) funds 9 AI Centres of Excellence, including healthcare, plus 6 bn THB for AI workforce development.
- PMU-B has already funded medical-AI data infrastructure, and PMU-C funds Chula robotics.
- The MHESI minister (Deputy PM Prof. Yodchanan Wongsawat) spoke on digital health and education infrastructure at BRIDGE-AI 2026.

Education research with students at Chula Medicine goes through the Faculty IRB, which offers Exempt and Expedited review. No explicit education-research exemption rule was confirmed.

### Inferences
- A capstone fits healthcare-AI workforce funding (the 6 bn THB AI workforce line and the healthcare CoE) and the PMU-B/PMU-C programs. Chula Medicine's own innovation division and the BRIDGE-AI competition offer near-term showcases.
- Student-participant studies will likely qualify for Exempt or Expedited review at the Chula MD IRB. Key risks are coercion (students recruited by their own teachers), keeping grades separate from participation, and video/biometric data under PDPA. Plan consent and anonymisation early and budget 1–2 months for IRB.
